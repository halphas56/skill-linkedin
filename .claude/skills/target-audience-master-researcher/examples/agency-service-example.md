# Worked Example — Agency Service

> **⚠ Constructed demonstration, not a case study.** The agency does not exist. Copy the reasoning; treat every finding as illustrative. `[A]` marks assumptions inserted to show how the analysis behaves.

---

## The brief as received

> "**Northline** — 4-person agency doing conversion-focused web design. £8–25k per project. Twelve months in, eleven projects, all from referrals and one founder's old network. We serve 'growing businesses that need a website that actually converts.' Referrals have dried up. Budget for acquisition: £500/month. Two of us can write; nobody wants to be on camera."

**Intake gaps:** no win/loss analysis, competitors described as "other agencies," no interviews, founder hours 10/week combined.

---

## 1. Offer summary

- **What is sold:** conversion-focused website redesign, £8–25k, 6–10 weeks
- **Job:** *When our website is visibly costing us deals we should be winning, I want it rebuilt by someone who can prove the change works, so I stop losing pipeline to a page.*
- **Genuinely better at:** shipping with measurement built in — every project ends with a before/after conversion baseline. Most design agencies deliver aesthetics and no number. `[A — needs verification with past clients]`
- **Model:** productised service · **Preset: Agency / service**
- **Regime:** **focus** — challenger, referral-dependent, no distribution

**On the stated audience.** "Growing businesses that need a website that actually converts" is a judgement Northline holds about them, not a circumstance they experience. No trigger, no vertical, no habitat. This is the standard agency failure: the audience is described by the *service*, not by the buyer's situation.

## 2. Key assumptions

| # | Assumption | If wrong | How to check | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The eleven past projects share an unnamed pattern | The whole ranking is inference | Win/loss + margin analysis on all 11 | 3 h |
| 2 | Measurement is a genuine differentiator, not table stakes | Positioning collapses to "nice design" | Ask 5 past clients why they chose Northline | 1 week |
| 3 | Trust threshold is clearable with a portfolio + one in-vertical case `[A]` | Sales cycle is far longer than modelled | Track what proof buyers ask for | ongoing |
| 4 | Referrals dried up because the source network was exhausted, not because of delivery quality | Marketing cannot fix a delivery problem | Ask 3 past clients if they'd refer, and why they haven't | 2 days |

**Confidence: low-medium.** Assumption 4 is uncomfortable and load-bearing. **An agency whose referrals stop should check its delivery before it buys traffic.** Three conversations settle it.

## 3. Audience universe map

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| User | Marketing manager | A site they can update | Being blamed for the old one | no | no |
| **Buyer** | Founder / commercial director | Pipeline that stops leaking | £20k spent, nothing changes | no | **yes** |
| Economic DM | Same person at this size | — | — | — | yes |
| Influencer | Whoever built the current site | To not be humiliated | Being replaced | **often** | no |
| Blocker | In-house dev or IT | No new stack | Losing control of the codebase | **yes** | no |

**Finding.** The **incumbent site-builder** — an in-house person, a freelancer on retainer, or the founder themselves — is an under-recognised blocker. A pitch that implies "your current site is bad" attacks a person who is often in the room. This changes the outreach language, not just the message.

## 5. Two segments in detail

### Segment 7 — Sales directors losing deals at the website

**Unexpected · Generator: C9 hidden B2B buyers · Evidence: `[S]`**

- **Description.** Commercial or sales directors in companies where prospects research online before a call, and the website actively undermines the pitch — outdated positioning, no proof, unclear offer.
- **Pain.** "My team gets the meeting and the prospect has already discounted us, because they looked us up first."
- **Trigger.** A lost deal where the prospect said something about the website; a new sales hire who complains; a competitor's site being visibly better.
- **Existing alternatives.** Complaining to marketing · a slide deck that compensates · nothing.
- **Ability to pay.** **High, and from a different pot.** Sales budgets are larger and less contested than marketing budgets in this size of company. `[A]`
- **Where they live.** Sales-leadership communities · LinkedIn sales content · sales conferences — **venues no design agency attends.**
- **Free channels.** Teardown content: "what your prospect sees before your call." Sales-community contribution.
- **Trust requirement.** Medium — they need proof of pipeline effect, not design awards.
- **Objections.** *Anxiety:* "isn't this marketing's job?" *Habit:* "we've always sold around the website."
- **Positioning hook.** *"Your prospects read your site before they take the call. Right now it's costing you the meeting."*
- **Why unexpected.** Every design agency markets to marketing. Sales is separately budgeted, more acutely pained, and completely unaddressed.
- **Risks.** Selling to sales requires speaking in pipeline terms, not design terms. A portfolio of pretty screenshots actively hurts here.

### Segment 8 — Manufacturers under distributor pressure

**Unexpected · Generator: C4 adjacent industries · Evidence: `[S]`**

- **Description.** Mid-sized manufacturers whose distributors or retail partners have started demanding better product presentation, or who are losing shelf position to better-presented competitors.
- **Pain.** Commercial and external: the distributor said so. This is not an internal preference — it is a partner requirement with consequences.
- **Ability to pay.** High. And critically, **the budget sits in sales or commercial, not marketing.**
- **Where they live.** Trade associations · trade shows (exhibitor lists are public and free to read) · industry press · distributor networks.
- **Free channels.** Trade-press articles · association contributions · speaking at a trade show · direct outreach referencing an exhibitor listing.
- **Trust requirement.** **High, and vertical-specific.** They will want a manufacturer case study. Northline has none — this is the gating constraint.
- **Positioning hook.** *"When your distributor says your product looks cheaper than it is."*
- **Risks.** Slow. Unfamiliar vertical. **Requires building the in-vertical proof asset first** — likely one project at a reduced fee in exchange for a public case.

### Reading it

**Segment 8 scores 3.90 — the second-highest total on the board — and is disqualified anyway.** Not because it is a bad audience: it has the best concentration, positioning and referral potential here. Because Northline **cannot currently clear its trust threshold.** They have no manufacturer case study, and manufacturers will ask for one.

Note what that does to the ranking. On totals alone, S8 (3.90) outranks S3 (3.81). **On gates, it is not eligible at all.** This is exactly why the matrix reports gates separately from totals, and why ranking by weighted average alone would have sent this agency into a vertical it cannot yet enter.

The correct output is not "reject" but *"excellent audience, currently closed to you, and here is the asset that opens it."* **A gate failure is a verdict on the fit between the segment and the business today — not on the segment.**

**Segment 1** fails on urgency and carries three assumptions. It is not a weak segment; it is not a segment.

**S7 and S8 sit 0.29 apart** — inside the tie band. The thing that separates them is not the score. It is that one is enterable this month and the other is not.

## 7. Top recommendations

**1. Segment 7 — Sales directors (4.19).** Uncontested (5 on competition), separately budgeted, acutely pained, and reachable via content Northline can actually produce in writing — which matters given nobody will go on camera. **Validate first:** that sales directors in this company size genuinely control budget of £8–25k.

**2. Segment 3 — First marketing hire (3.81).** Best reachability on the board (5): job postings are public, dated, and self-replenishing. Free acquisition is strong. **Validate first:** whether a new hire can authorise this spend in their first quarter, or whether they can only recommend.

**3. Segment 8 — Manufacturers (3.90, gate-blocked).** Higher-scoring than S3 and still ranked below it, because eligibility beats score. Named as a **strategic target, not a current one.** The unlock is one manufacturer case study. Recommended path: take one at a reduced fee explicitly in exchange for a public, numbers-included case study, then revisit. This is a deliberate investment, not a discount.

**Anti-audience: pre-revenue startups and "just needs a refresh" projects.** 4 of 11 past projects, lowest margin, most scope creep `[A]`. Framed for the client in resource terms: *"these four consumed the capacity that would have delivered two full projects."*

## 9. Free acquisition

**Verdict: realistic.** Two writers, two dense habitats, and a category where demonstration beats assertion.

| Channel | Segment | Asset | Time-to-signal | Fails if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Public teardowns** | S7, S11 | Real expertise + judgement about tone | **2–6 weeks** | It punches down, or shows cleverness instead of method |
| Sales-community contribution | S7 | Credibility in pipeline language | 4–8 weeks | Talks about design instead of deals |
| Trigger outreach on job postings | S3 | List + reason to write | 1–3 weeks | Generic, or implies the new hire's site is bad |
| Comparison / "how to brief an agency" content | all | Writing capacity | 3–6 months | Written for peers rather than buyers |
| Partnerships with non-competing agencies (PPC, SEO) | S11 | Something to trade | 3–8 weeks | Partner chosen by size not overlap |

**Teardowns are the highest-leverage asset here** and deserve a specific rule: **tear down a category or a volunteer, never a named company that did not ask.** Public criticism of an unwilling subject makes an enemy of the exact buyer persona you are courting — and every prospect quietly wonders whether they are next.

**Constraint:** 10 combined founder-hours/week supports **two** channels. Recommend teardowns + trigger outreach.

## 10. Paid acquisition

- **Reality first:** £500/month cannot run a decision-grade test on LinkedIn, which is the only platform that can express these segments. Say this plainly rather than designing a campaign that will produce unreadable data.
- **What £500/month *can* do:** retargeting readers of the teardown content, and a small LinkedIn test against **one** segment and **one** angle at a time, run sequentially.
- **Targeting:** S7 — sales director titles, company size 20–200. S3 — marketing titles, tenure under 6 months.
- **Proxy warning:** you are buying a title, not a circumstance. Expect substantial leakage.
- **ARPU ↔ CAC:** at £8–25k per project the CAC ceiling is generous — the constraint is not affordability but **statistical power at £500/month.**
- **Recommendation:** treat paid as an amplifier for content that already works organically. Do not use it to discover the segment; use free channels for that, which at this budget are strictly better instruments.

## 11. Positioning — Segment 7

- **Competitive alternatives:** the in-house marketer redoing it · a freelancer · a cheaper agency · doing nothing and selling around the site
- **Unique attribute:** every project ships with a measured conversion baseline
- **Differentiated value:** you can prove the site stopped costing you deals, rather than believing it looks better
- **Best-fit customer:** sales leaders whose pipeline is measurably affected and who are judged on a number
- **Category:** conversion-focused web design — existing category, narrowed by buyer

**Statement:**
> *For sales directors whose prospects research the company before the call, Northline rebuilds the site around what the buyer is actually checking — and measures the change in booked meetings. Unlike design agencies that hand over a beautiful site and no number, we start from your pipeline data and end there too.*

**Swap test:** substitute a well-known design agency → "we start from your pipeline data and end there too" reads false. **Passes.**

**Declared falsifier:** if test 2 reveals past clients are lukewarm about the work, this is a delivery problem, and no audience strategy fixes it. **The correct output in that case is to say so, not to proceed with a campaign.**

## 13. Final recommendation

1. **Best audience to start with:** **sales directors losing deals at the website** — uncontested, separately budgeted, and reachable through writing.
2. **Backup:** **first marketing hires** — best reachability, promote if sales-budget ownership proves rare.
3. **Strategic target, currently blocked:** **manufacturers under distributor pressure** — unlocked by one in-vertical case study. Worth taking one project at a reduced fee to obtain it.
4. **Audience to avoid:** pre-revenue startups and refresh jobs.
5. **First experiment:** three candid calls with past clients about why they have not referred anyone. Two days.
6. **First message angle:** *"Your prospects read your site before they take the call."*
7. **First channel:** public teardowns, distributed into sales-leadership communities.

**Trade-off accepted:** positioning for sales directors means the portfolio has to be rebuilt around pipeline outcomes rather than visual craft. For a design team, that is genuinely uncomfortable — the work that wins this segment is not the work they are proudest of.

**What would make this wrong:** if past clients reveal that referrals stopped because of delivery rather than network exhaustion. **That is why test 2 runs first, before any positioning work.**

## Self-critique

- **Weakest part:** assumption 2. The entire differentiation rests on measurement data that may not exist in usable form.
- **Could not verify:** whether sales budgets at this company size stretch to £8–25k for a website. It is the pivot of the top recommendation.
- **Reasoned by analogy:** budget-ownership patterns, marked `[A]`.
- **A sceptic would attack:** recommending a segment (S7) whose venues the agency has never entered, on the strength of reasoning rather than evidence. Fair. Test 4 exists for exactly that.
- **Next, given one more day:** tests 1 and 2 together — five hours, and they gate everything else.

## ⚠ Delta to the current method

**Written in research pass 1; predates passes 3–6.** Reference implementation: [`00-reference-engagement.md`](00-reference-engagement.md).

What the current method would add **for Northline specifically**:

| Instrument | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Market map** | Eleven projects, **all from referrals** — that makes the referrer a **leverage junction**, not a marketing channel. Quantify it: what share of decisions is made by the person recommending Northline rather than by the client? For a referral-only agency this is frequently the majority, and it is entirely unaddressed here |
| **Commoditisation diagnostic** | Agencies reliably report *"clients only compare price."* Treat that as a hypothesis about the agency's own research. The ICI figure — 10% genuinely price-led against 100% believed — is the reason |
| **Trust accessibility as a gate** | For a four-person agency, any segment requiring procurement, security review or references-in-vertical is **structurally closed today**, whatever it scores. That is a gate failure, not a deduction |
| **Anti-ICP with numbers** | Frame refusal in resource terms: which of the eleven projects consumed disproportionate margin, support and calendar. *"X% of revenue, Y% of hours"* — never a judgement about clients |
| **Economic value estimation** | Price against the client's problem, not against Northline's day rate. A conversion redesign touching £2m of revenue justifies a different fee from the same work touching £200k — same deliverable, different segment |
