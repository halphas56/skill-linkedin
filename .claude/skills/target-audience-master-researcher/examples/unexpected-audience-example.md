# Worked Example — Unexpected Audience Discovery

> **⚠ Constructed demonstration, not a case study.** The company does not exist. This example exists to show **the discovery process working step by step** — how the generators fire, what evidence looks like, and how a non-obvious segment is disqualified or promoted. `[A]` marks assumptions inserted to show how the analysis behaves.

---

## The brief as received

> "**Fieldnote** — waterproof all-weather notebooks and pressurised pens. £18 per notebook, £24 for a set. We sell direct-to-consumer to hikers, climbers and campers. Two years in, £14k/month revenue, flat for eight months. Meta ads used to work and now don't — the outdoor market is saturated and CPMs have doubled. We think we need better creative."

**The client's diagnosis:** a creative problem.
**The hypothesis this example tests:** it is a **segment** problem, and the answer is already sitting in their own review data.

---

## Part 1 — The discovery run

This section is the point of the example. The analysis comes after.

---

### Step 1 — Establish the job, strip the industry

**As sold:** "notebooks for hikers."
**As a job:** *When I have to record something accurate in conditions that destroy paper, I want a record that survives, so I don't lose information I can't recreate.*

**Strip the outdoor context from that sentence.** Nothing in it mentions hiking. That is the first signal: the product was scoped by the founder's own hobby, not by the job.

> **Generator C4 (adjacent industries with the same job) is now live.** *Who else records things in conditions that destroy paper?*

---

### Step 2 — Read your own reviews for the wrong customer

**Method:** review mining, concentrating on 3- and 4-star reviews, flagging any reviewer who is clearly not the intended customer.

Fictional but representative sample of what an anomaly hunt surfaces across ~120 own-product reviews:

| Reviewer signal | Count `[A]` | Verbatim shape |
|---|---|---|
| Hiking / camping (intended) | 71 | "great for the Lakes in November" |
| **Trade / worksite** | **16** | "site notes in the rain, finally something that survives a hi-vis pocket" |
| **Marine / boat** | **9** | "chart notes without the clipboard blowing away" |
| **Kitchen** | **6** | "prep notes near the wash station — normal pads turn to mush" |
| **Veterinary / farm** | **5** | "farm calls, gloves, disinfectant" |
| **Aviation maintenance** | **4** | "hangar use, oil doesn't wreck it" |
| Gift purchases | 9 | — |

**The finding.** Roughly **a third of reviewers are not hiking.** They are working. Nobody at Fieldnote had counted this, because nobody had read reviews looking for *who wrote them* rather than *what they said about the product*.

> **This is the single highest-yield move in the whole example, and it cost two hours.**

---

### Step 3 — Run the remaining generators

Each generator is a question aimed at the market. Run them all; do not pre-judge.

| Gen | Question | What it surfaced |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** Who built a workaround? | Who hacks a solution to this today? | Photos in forum threads of laminated index cards, notes written on tape stuck to a forearm, phone-in-a-freezer-bag, dictating voice memos and transcribing later |
| **C2** Extreme users | Who does this 10× more than normal? | Commercial divers; offshore crews; anyone whose entire shift is documentation in a hostile environment |
| **C3** Wrong customer in competitor reviews | Who complains in the *competitor's* reviews? | "Fine for camping, but the pages are too small for a job sheet" — a specification request from a segment the competitor also isn't serving |
| **C4** Adjacent industries | Same job, unrelated industry | Construction · marine survey · agriculture · aviation maintenance · forensics · commercial kitchens · utilities field crews |
| **C6** Refusers | Who evaluated and said no? | Site managers who looked and concluded "too small, too expensive per unit for a crew of 40" — **a packaging objection, not a product objection** |
| **C9** Hidden B2B buyer | Who pays when the user doesn't? | **The company.** A site supervisor doesn't buy their own notebook — procurement or the safety officer does |
| **C10** High urgency, low saturation | Dated, penalised, unglamorous | **Regulated record-keeping**: site safety records, aircraft maintenance sign-off, food-safety logs, evidence handling |
| **C11** Multiple pressures | Two or more of compliance/time/risk/status/money | Construction and aviation carry **compliance + risk** simultaneously |
| **C12** Repeat questions | Asked monthly, no supplier | "What do you all use for site notes in the wet?" recurring in trade forums `[A]` |

**Candidates generated: 11.** Six from outside the outdoor market.

---

### Step 4 — Apply the four disqualification tests

Novelty is not a score. Every candidate is tested before it reaches a recommendation.

| Candidate | List test | Money test | Absence test | Reach-cost test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Construction site supervisors** | ✅ trade associations, site-safety groups, wholesalers | ✅ already buy PPE, site consumables, safety kit | ✅ *"nobody thought to look"* — outdoor brands don't sell to trade | ✅ trade wholesalers and forums are open | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| **Aviation maintenance** | ✅ licensed, registered, associated | ✅ established consumables budget | ⚠️ possibly **certification requirements** for records | ⚠️ gatekept | **HYPOTHESIS — check certification first** |
| **Commercial kitchens** | ✅ trade suppliers, chef communities | ⚠️ notoriously thin margins | ✅ nobody looks | ✅ cheap to reach | **HYPOTHESIS** |
| **Marine survey / offshore** | ✅ small, licensed, listed | ✅ high budgets | ✅ nobody looks | ✅ tiny and reachable | **RECOMMENDATION — small but rich** |
| **Forensics / evidence handling** | ✅ institutional | ✅ public budgets | ❌ **evidence-integrity requirements likely mandate specific certified products** | ❌ procurement | **DISQUALIFIED — the absence has a reason** |
| **Veterinary farm calls** | ✅ associations, registers | ✅ practice budgets | ✅ nobody looks | ✅ | **HYPOTHESIS** |
| **Divers (extreme users)** | ✅ tiny, dense | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | **RECOMMENDATION as a proof segment, not a revenue segment** |

**Note what the absence test did.** Forensics looked like the most exciting candidate on the list — public budgets, extreme pain, zero competition. The test asked *why* nobody serves them and returned a reason: **evidence-handling requirements probably mandate specified, certified materials.** That is a wall, not an opening. Ten minutes of asking "why not?" saved a quarter of misdirected effort.

**Note what the money test did to kitchens.** Real pain, real reach, thin margins — demoted to hypothesis, not rejected.

---

### Step 5 — The structural realisation

Look at what the surviving candidates share:

- The **user** is an individual worker.
- The **buyer** is the employer — procurement, a site manager, or a safety officer.
- The purchase is **not one notebook. It is forty.**
- The trigger is **compliance**, not preference.

**The consequence is larger than a targeting change.** Fieldnote is not a D2C brand with an underexploited segment. It is a **B2B consumables business** that has been selling one unit at a time to the hobbyist end of its own market, at the highest possible CAC, in the most saturated channel available.

That is why the ads stopped working. It was never a creative problem.

### Reading it

**The current audience fails a gate.** Hikers score 2.58 and fail on urgency — a hiker who forgets their notebook has a mildly annoying walk. There is no dated consequence, which is precisely why they are expensive to acquire and do not repeat.

**Retention is the tell.** Hikers score 2; every trade segment scores 5. A construction firm with forty crew buys notebooks **on a schedule, forever.** A hiker buys one notebook and, if the product is good, never buys another. **Fieldnote's product quality is actively working against its business model in the consumer segment.**

**Competition inverts completely.** Hikers: 1 (saturated, CPMs doubled). Trade: 5 (nobody is there).

**Both weightings produce the same ordering**, which strengthens the conclusion — the ranking is not an artefact of preset choice. Where they differ, the B2B weighting most benefits **marine survey (+0.30)**, whose value is concentrated precisely in reachability and referral, the two criteria that weighting elevates. Note also that hikers rise slightly (+0.12) under B2B weights, because that preset discounts paid acquisition — a criterion on which hikers score 2. **Neither preset rescues them, which is the point.**

**Assumption counts are high (3–4) for the new segments.** This is honest and important: these are recommendations built on review-mining inference and reasoning, not on customer conversations. **The validation plan exists to close that gap before money is spent.**

## 7. Top recommendations

**1. Construction site supervisors and safety officers (4.16 / 4.27)**
Attractive because compliance supplies both urgency and budget: site records must exist, in the rain, and someone is accountable if they do not. Reachable through trade wholesalers, site-safety communities and associations — none of which any outdoor brand touches. Retention is structural: consumables bought for a crew, repeatedly. Positioning is unclaimed.
**Validate first:** that Fieldnote can produce a **crew-pack** at a trade unit price. The refusers already told us the objection — *"too small, too expensive per unit for a crew of 40"* — and it is a packaging and pricing objection, not a product one.

**2. Marine survey and offshore crews (4.04 / 4.34)**
Smaller, richer, denser. Highest willingness to pay and highest referral potential of anything generated — it is a tiny professional world where everyone knows everyone. Best used as the **proof segment**: win a handful, get a named reference, use it to open construction.

**3. Commercial kitchens (3.52 / 3.46)** — hypothesis. Real pain, cheap to reach, thin margins. Test only after the trade packaging exists.

**Named and disqualified: forensics.** Highest apparent opportunity, disqualified by the absence test on likely certification requirements. **Stated explicitly in the deliverable**, because a client who sees the same opportunity later deserves to know it was examined and why it was set aside.

**Anti-audience: gift buyers.** ~9 of 120 reviews. Zero retention, highest expectation of packaging and presentation, seasonal, and they distort the product roadmap toward aesthetics. Serve them if they arrive; never optimise for them.

## 8. Where to find them

| Habitat | Segment | Match | Route in | Free? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trade wholesalers and site-supply catalogues | Construction | **High** | Distribution conversation — one relationship reaches hundreds of sites | Partly |
| Site-safety and construction-management communities | Construction | High | Answer the recurring "what do you use in the wet" question | **Yes** |
| Trade associations, member directories | Construction, Marine | High | Public directories; member publications accept contributions | **Yes** |
| Trade shows — **exhibitor and speaker lists** | All trade | **High** | Read the lists without attending; a free map of the segment | **Yes** |
| Marine survey and offshore professional bodies | Marine | **High** | Small, licensed, listed | **Yes** |
| Trade forums and subreddits | All | Med | Contribute; never pitch | **Yes** |
| Existing reviewers who are already trade | All | **Highest** | **Email the 34 non-hiker reviewers and ask them what they do** | **Yes** |

**The Friday test.** *To reach 100 of them by Friday:* email the 34 trade reviewers already in the database (highest-quality warm list in existence — they already bought), and post one honest answer in three trade communities.

**Note that the best habitat was already owned.** Thirty-four self-identified trade users were sitting in the review data, unread, for two years.

## 9. Free acquisition

**Verdict: realistic — and cheaper than the current paid motion, which is failing.**

| Channel | Asset | Time-to-signal | Fails if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Email the 34 trade reviewers** | A list that already exists | **Days** | Written as a sales email instead of a research question |
| Trade community contribution | Someone who can speak to site conditions credibly | 3–6 weeks | An outdoor brand voice; trades detect it instantly |
| Wholesaler / distributor outreach | A trade-priced crew pack | 4–10 weeks | The pack doesn't exist yet |
| Association member publications | A written case, ideally with a named site | 6–12 weeks | No customer will be named |
| Trade-show exhibitor list outreach | List reading + outreach | 2–4 weeks | Generic messaging |

**First action, and it is nearly free:** write to the 34. Not to sell — to ask what they do, why they bought, whether their employer would buy in bulk, and what would have to change. That single email is simultaneously the cheapest research, the cheapest validation, and the warmest outreach available.

---

## 10. Paid acquisition

- **Stop increasing spend on outdoor targeting.** The scoring explains why: competition 1, urgency 1, retention 2. The creative is not the problem — the segment is exhausted and the product does not repeat there.
- **Trade segments are not primarily a paid-ads game.** They are reached through **distribution and outbound**: wholesalers, associations, trade shows. One wholesaler relationship reaches more sites than a month of ad spend.
- **Where paid does apply:** search ads on trade-intent queries (*"waterproof site notebook,"* *"all weather job sheets"*) — low volume, very high intent. And LinkedIn against safety-officer titles, if crew-pack pricing exists.
- **Targeting reality:** no platform can target "site supervisor who documents in the rain." Search intent is the closest expressible proxy, and it is a good one — intent beats demographics.
- **ARPU ↔ CAC:** the arithmetic transforms with the crew pack. £18 one-off versus a recurring forty-unit order is a different business with a different affordable CAC.
- **What would falsify:** if trade-intent search volume is negligible **and** wholesalers decline, the trade route requires field sales — which this business cannot currently fund. That would be a genuine finding, not a failure.

## 11. Positioning — construction

- **Competitive alternatives:** ordinary site pads that disintegrate · laminated cards · phone photos · a plastic wallet · writing on tape · not recording it at all
- **Unique attribute:** paper and ink that survive rain, mud, oil and a hi-vis pocket
- **Differentiated value:** the record exists when someone asks for it
- **Best-fit customer:** whoever is accountable when a site record is missing
- **Category:** **site consumables**, not outdoor gear. The category change *is* the strategy — it moves the product from a discretionary purchase to a budgeted one.

**Statement:**
> *For site supervisors who have to produce records that were written in the rain, Fieldnote is site stationery that survives the site — notebooks and pens that don't fail in mud, oil or a wet pocket. Unlike ordinary site pads, the record is still legible when someone asks for it three weeks later.*

**Swap test:** substitute a general stationery supplier → *"survives the site"* reads false. **Passes.**

**Proof needed:** `[PROOF NEEDED: one named site or contractor willing to be quoted. The 34 existing trade reviewers are the candidate pool — start there.]`

**Note the honest constraint:** the phrase "compliance" is doing real work in this positioning and must be used carefully. Fieldnote is not selling compliance; it sells stationery that survives conditions. **Claiming to satisfy a regulatory requirement it has not been certified against would be both false and dangerous** — this is exactly the line that disqualified the forensics segment.

## 12. Validation plan

| # | Hypothesis | Method | Success metric | Min. evidence | Cost | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A third of buyers are trade | Recount all reviews + order data by occupation signal | % non-hiking | Clear pattern | Free | **2 h** |
| 2 | Trade users would buy in bulk via employer | **Email the 34; ask, don't sell** | Replies confirming employer purchase | 8 replies, 3 confirming | Free | **1 wk** |
| 3 | The refuser objection is packaging, not product | Ask the same 34 what stopped a bulk order | Objection named | 5 answers | Free | included |
| 4 | Trade-intent search volume exists | Keyword research on trade phrasing | Non-trivial volume with ads present | Any meaningful volume | Free | 2 h |
| 5 | Wholesalers will stock it | 10 approaches with a crew-pack concept | Meetings | 2 meetings | Free | 3 wk |
| 6 | Crew-pack economics work | Cost the 40-unit pack | Margin at trade price | Positive margin | Free | 1 wk |
| 7 | Trade message beats outdoor message | Two landing pages, identical structure, search traffic | Cost per enquiry | 2× difference | £400 | 3 wk |
| 8 | Marine segment is real | 10 approaches via professional bodies | Replies | 3 replies | Free | 2 wk |

**Run order:** 1 and 4 today (four hours). 2 and 3 this week — **one email, three answers.** Then 5, 6, 8. Test 7 last, and only if the crew pack exists.

**Declared falsifier:** if the 34 trade reviewers reply that they buy personally and their employer would never stock it, the B2B thesis collapses and the correct move is a trade-oriented D2C position instead — same segment, different model.

## 13. Final recommendation

1. **Best audience to start with:** **construction site supervisors and safety officers** — compliance-driven, budgeted, uncontested, and structurally repeat-purchase.
2. **Proof segment:** **marine survey and offshore** — small, dense, high willingness to pay, generates the named reference that opens construction.
3. **Hypothesis to park:** commercial kitchens.
4. **Disqualified, and say so:** forensics — the absence has a reason.
5. **Audience to avoid optimising for:** gift buyers.
6. **First experiment:** email the 34 trade reviewers already in the database. This week. Cost: an hour.
7. **First message angle:** *"The record is still legible when someone asks for it three weeks later."*
8. **First channel:** the existing customer list, then trade wholesalers.

**Trade-off accepted:** this reframes Fieldnote from an outdoor brand into a trade-consumables supplier. The brand becomes less romantic and the business becomes viable. A founder who started this because they love hiking will find that genuinely hard — and it should be named out loud rather than buried in a recommendation.

**What would make this wrong:** if the trade reviewers are all sole traders buying personally, with no route to employer purchasing. That is one email away from being known.

## What this example is meant to teach

1. **The answer was in data they already owned.** Two years of reviews, unread for *who wrote them*. Before commissioning research, read what you already have.
2. **"The creative isn't working" is usually a segment diagnosis wearing a creative costume.** Saturated CPMs in an exhausted segment are a market signal, not a design brief.
3. **The absence test earns its place.** Forensics looked like the biggest prize on the board and was correctly set aside in ten minutes.
4. **An unexpected audience can change the business model, not just the targeting.** D2C to B2B consumables is a different company. Say that plainly rather than presenting it as a channel tweak.
5. **Retention exposed the real problem.** A product good enough to last forever cannot sustain a consumer repeat business. The same durability that scores 2 with hikers scores 5 with a crew of forty.
6. **Assumption counts stayed high, and were reported.** Three to four assumption cells per new segment is honest for a review-mining-based analysis. The validation plan exists to retire them before money moves.

## ⚠ Delta to the current method

**Written in research pass 1; predates passes 3–6.** Its teaching point — discovery from the client's own review data — remains current. What the instrument set would add for Fieldnote:

| Instrument | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Reviews ≠ comments** | **Reviews contain buyers only.** If the unexpected audience includes people who considered the product and did not buy, review mining cannot see them by construction. Add comment mining and marketplace pre-purchase questions to reach non-buyers |
| **Evidence ceiling** | A finding drawn entirely from review data is `L3` — one source type, however many reviews. It reaches `L4` only when a **different kind** of source agrees: search data, a job posting, a completed transaction elsewhere |
| **Named generators** | The discovery here is **T3 (different job)** and **T10 (unusual uses)** from the Different-X matrix, plus Christensen's *unusual uses* hunting ground — the Arm & Hammer pattern. Naming the generator makes the process repeatable rather than lucky |
| **Nonconsumption check** | If the category looks saturated, the job may be defined poorly. Size the *struggle* population against the *buying* population; the gap is the segment |
| **The disqualification tests** | Novelty is not a score. Run all four: the list test · the money test · the absence test (*why does no competitor serve them?*) · the reachability-cost test. An unexpected segment failing one is a hypothesis with an experiment attached, not a recommendation |
