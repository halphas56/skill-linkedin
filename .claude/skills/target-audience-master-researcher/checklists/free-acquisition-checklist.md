# Free Acquisition Checklist

**Question this answers:** can this audience realistically be reached without an advertising budget — and if so, through what, at what cost in time, and by when?

**The premise.** There is no free acquisition. There is acquisition paid for in **time, credibility and optionality** instead of cash. The honest question is never "is it free?" but **"which currency can this business actually spend, and how long can it wait for a signal?"**

---

## Step 3 — Match channel to situation

Run in order. **Stop at the first honest yes.** Running all seven produces a menu, not a plan.

### 1. Is the segment gathered somewhere dense?
→ **Community participation + content in that venue.** The cheapest real acquisition there is.
- Required asset: genuine expertise + a real account with history + patience
- Time-to-signal: **2–8 weeks**
- Compounds: partially
- First action: join, read for two weeks, answer three questions with no link
- **Fails if:** the founder cannot answer questions credibly, or vendors are banned

### 2. Is the segment listable with observable triggers?
→ **Cold outreach on trigger events.** Free, fast signal, immediate learning.
- Required asset: a list, a reason to write, tolerance for silence, legal compliance
- Time-to-signal: **1–3 weeks**
- Compounds: no
- First action: 30 named people, one message each referencing a specific observable fact
- **Fails if:** you cannot build the list. *(30 well-researched messages with zero replies is a reachability verdict, not a copy problem.)*

### 3. Does the segment search for the problem?
→ **Content + SEO.**
- Required asset: subject expertise, a site, publishing capacity
- Time-to-signal: **4–12 months**
- Compounds: **yes — strongly**
- First action: the repeat question from Step 2, answered better than anything currently ranking
- **Fails if:** runway is shorter than the signal, or the domain cannot compete on the terms that matter

### 4. Does the founder have standing in this segment?
→ **Founder-led content + the sales-call flywheel.** Sales calls generate the topics; content generates the inbound; the loop compounds.
- Required asset: founder time and willingness to be visible
- Time-to-signal: **4–12 weeks**
- Compounds: yes
- First action: publish the answer to the question the last three sales calls all asked
- **Fails if:** delegated to a ghostwriter with no access to the founder's actual thinking — audiences detect the hollowness

### 5. Does someone else already own this audience?
→ **Partnerships · guest publishing · co-hosted sessions.**
- Required asset: something the partner wants — audience, product, expertise, or their client's problem solved
- Time-to-signal: **3–8 weeks per partnership**
- Compounds: no
- First action: list ten non-competing businesses selling to the identical buyer; offer value first
- **Fails if:** you have nothing to offer yet, or you selected by audience *size* rather than *overlap*

### 6. Do satisfied customers exist?
→ **Referral, asked properly.** The only channel with negative marginal cost.
- Required asset: a moment of proven value, and a specific ask
- Time-to-signal: **immediate**
- Compounds: yes, if the segment talks
- First action: at the next visible win, ask for **two named introductions** — not a general referral
- **Fails if:** asked before value is proven, which damages the relationship you are borrowing

### 7. None of the above, and there is no budget?
→ **The segment is closed to you today.** State it plainly and name either the asset to build or the segment to change to. This is a legitimate and frequently correct conclusion.

---

## Step 4 — Reality gates

Before recommending any free channel, all five must pass.

- [ ] **Time-to-signal < runway.** Signal: ___ weeks · Runway: ___ weeks
- [ ] **Required asset exists, or its build time is included in the plan**
- [ ] **The person who will do the work has agreed to do it** — not "we should start a blog," but *who*, on *which day*
- [ ] **Venue rules checked, not assumed**
- [ ] **Cadence is sustainable at the stated founder hours** — half the recommended cadence, sustained, beats the full one abandoned in month three

**Any gate failing → downgrade the recommendation and say why.**

---

## Step 5 — Predictable failure conditions

Free acquisition fails predictably here. Predicting it is more valuable to the client than a channel list.

| Condition | Why free fails | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Audience unaware of the problem | You fund three rungs of education | Narrow to a problem-aware sub-segment, or budget for the education |
| No dense venue | Content reaches statistical noise | Paid targeting, or change segment |
| Founder has no time and will not be public | The required currency is unavailable | Paid, or outsourced outbound on a bought list |
| Purchase is rare and impulse-driven | No compounding surface to build on | Search and marketplace presence only |
| Category dominated by high-authority incumbents | The organic ceiling is set by their domain and their years | Attack a sub-segment they ignore, or buy the traffic |
| Runway under three months | Every free channel's signal exceeds the runway | Paid or outbound, immediately |
| Community explicitly bans vendors | The only compliant move is absence | Partner with a member, or sponsor officially |
| Audience consumes video; nobody will be on camera | Format mismatch, rarely stated, routinely fatal | Change format, change person, or change channel |

---

## The honest framing to give the client

> Free acquisition is not cheaper. It is **paid for in founder time and credibility instead of cash**, and it usually pays back later and larger. Paid acquisition buys speed and certainty and stops the moment you stop paying. Most businesses that succeed on "free" channels spent more, in the currency they had, than the ad budget would have cost — and ended up owning an asset instead of renting attention.
>
> The question is not which is better. It is which currency you have, and how long you can wait.
