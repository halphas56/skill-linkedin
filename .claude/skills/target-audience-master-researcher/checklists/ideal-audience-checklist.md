# Ideal Audience Checklist

**Use:** run against any candidate segment before recommending it. Takes about ten minutes per segment once you have the research.

**Scoring:** tick only what you have **evidence** for. A box ticked on intuition is worse than an empty box, because an empty box tells you what to research next.

---

## Tier 0 — The gates (fail one, stop here)

A segment failing any gate is not a weak segment. It is **not a segment**, and no strength elsewhere compensates.

- [ ] **G1 · Painful problem.** They name the problem themselves, unprompted, in public. There is vocabulary for it.
  - Evidence: ______________________
  - *Fail if:* you must explain the problem before you can sell the solution.

- [ ] **G2 · Urgency.** Something bad happens on a date if nothing is done.
  - The date / event: ______________________
  - *Fail if:* the honest answer to "what happens if you do nothing for six months?" is "nothing much."

- [ ] **G3 · Ability to pay.** A budget already exists — a line item, a vendor being paid, a salary larger than your price.
  - Evidence: ______________________
  - *Fail if:* the segment's defining feature is that it cannot afford the incumbent.

- [ ] **G4 · Reachability.** You can name a place where 200 of them are already gathered, listed or indexed.
  - The place: ______________________
  - *Fail if:* the segment is defined by an internal state with no external marker.

- [ ] **G5 · Trust path.** The proof this segment accepts is proof you can produce within your runway.
  - Proof required: ______________ · Can produce it in: ______________
  - *Fail if:* they need certifications, references or procurement clearance you cannot obtain in time.

**Gates passed: ___ / 5.** Below 5, stop and report the failure. *"Excellent audience, currently closed to you, here is the asset that would open it"* is a complete professional answer.

## Tier 1 — Demand quality

- [ ] **Frequency.** The triggering circumstance recurred in the last 12 months. How often: ______
- [ ] **Willingness to pay** (separate from ability). They already pay for something comparable, without drama.
  - What they already pay for: ______________
- [ ] **Existing spending behaviour.** There is an incumbent being displaced, not a budget being created from nothing.
- [ ] **Existing alternatives named.** Including "nothing," "a spreadsheet," "an intern." You know what you actually lose to.
- [ ] **Dissatisfaction evidenced.** Consistent complaints about the same dimension across multiple providers.
- [ ] **Existing search behaviour** *(skip if new market)*. Real query volume, with commercial modifiers, and ads on the SERP.
- [ ] **Trigger events identified.** Externally observable and timestamped.
  - Triggers: ______________________

---

## Tier 2 — Reachability quality

- [ ] **Concentration.** Three venues cover most of the segment.
- [ ] **Community density.** They know each other. Referral loops exist without you.
- [ ] **Content consumption pattern known** — and it matches what this business can actually produce.
  - They consume: ______ · We can produce: ______ · **Match?** yes / no
- [ ] **At least two viable channels**, at least one of them free or nearly free.
- [ ] **Both channels testable within 30 days** on the available budget.
- [ ] **The Friday test passes.** *"To reach 100 of them by Friday we would ___."* Answer: ______________

---

## Tier 3 — Economic quality

- [ ] **Countable.** You can estimate how many exist, bottom-up.  Estimate: ______
- [ ] **Model-market arithmetic clears.** price × reachable customers × plausible capture ≥ what the business needs.
- [ ] **ARPU funds the channel.** The price supports the CAC that the only available channel demands.
- [ ] **Repeat purchase or referral present.** At least one of the two — a segment with neither forces you into pure acquisition economics, the hardest game there is.
- [ ] **Sales cycle fits the cash position.** Cycle length: ______ · Runway: ______

---

## Tier 4 — Strategic quality

- [ ] **Founder / brand credibility fits.** Shared background, prior experience, or an existing network in this segment.
  - People already known in this segment: ______
- [ ] **The team would enjoy this for three years.** *(Aulet's values-and-passions criterion. Segments chosen purely from a spreadsheet die in month four from low reply rates and boredom.)*
- [ ] **Positioning opportunity exists.** You can say something true that no competitor can say honestly.
  - The claim: ______________________
- [ ] **Swap test passes.** Substituting the strongest competitor's name makes the positioning read false.
- [ ] **Incumbents cannot follow** without breaking their own positioning.
- [ ] **Leverage into adjacent segments** exists — winning here opens somewhere else.
- [ ] **Low education cost.** They are solution-aware or better. Awareness stage: ______

---

## Tier 5 — Definitional quality

The segment definition itself must survive inspection.

- [ ] Contains a **who**
- [ ] Contains a **when** (circumstance or trigger) — *without this it is a list, not demand*
- [ ] Contains a **where** (habitat) — *without this it is a wish, not a plan*
- [ ] **Homogeneity holds:** same product · same sales process · word of mouth between them
- [ ] **The anti-audience is named** — you know who inside this group to refuse
- [ ] **A different segment would find the promise surprising** *(if not, the definition is too broad)*
- [ ] **Demographics appear only as targeting parameters**, never as the definition

---

## Tier 6 — Evidence quality

- [ ] Every claim traces to an interview, review, query, thread, transaction or observable artefact
- [ ] Verbatim language captured — you can quote them
- [ ] Assumptions marked and counted. Count: ___ / 19 matrix cells
- [ ] At least one piece of **external** evidence exists (not just the founder's belief)
- [ ] The load-bearing assumption is identified, with the cost of checking it
- [ ] Counter-evidence sought — you looked for reasons this segment is wrong, not only reasons it is right

**Reading:**

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Gates < 5 | **Disqualified.** Report the gate and the condition that would open it. |
| Gates 5, total 35+ | **Strong.** Proceed to positioning and a validation experiment. |
| Gates 5, total 25–34 | **Viable.** Proceed, and name the unticked Tier 1–3 boxes as what to test first. |
| Gates 5, total < 25 | **Under-researched, not necessarily weak.** The output is a research plan, not a recommendation. |
| Tier 6 below 4 | **Do not recommend regardless of the rest.** You have a hypothesis with confident formatting. |

## The ten-second version

When there is no time for 43 boxes:

> **Massive pain · purchasing power · easy to target · growing.**

Then one more question the compressed version omits and the corpus insists on:

> **And can we be the obvious choice here, or just one of nine reasonable ones?**
