## How to read this file

This is **a representative corpus, not an exhaustive survey.** Nobody read "everything on the internet" about audience research. What follows is a deliberately diverse set of sources — primary framework authors, practitioner blogs, Russian-language marketing media, and tool documentation — chosen to cover the eight clusters below with at least two independent sources per cluster wherever possible.

Every entry carries a **Depth** marker. This matters for how much weight a claim should carry:

| Depth marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FULL** | The page was retrieved and read in full during this research pass. |
| **SNIPPET** | Only search-result extracts were reviewed. Framework name, author and headline claims are confirmed; fine detail is not. |
| **BLOCKED** | Retrieval was attempted and failed (connection refused, 403). Listed for transparency; **not** used as a citation for any specific claim. |
| **PRIOR** | Established, widely-documented work known independently of this pass. Cited by name and book/article, with a corroborating URL where one was found. |

**Reliability** is scored 1–5 on: is this the originator or a faithful secondary source; is the claim falsifiable; is the source selling something that biases the claim.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Primary author / original publication of the framework. |
| 4 | High-quality secondary source, faithful to the original, practitioner-tested. |
| 3 | Competent practitioner content; useful but generic or partly promotional. |
| 2 | Vendor content marketing; the framework is real but the framing sells a product. |
| 1 | Aggregator / SEO filler. Used only for terminology, never for claims. |

## Cluster 1 — Market & segment selection

### 1.1 Beachhead Market (Step 2 of 24)
- **Author / org:** Bill Aulet / MIT Martin Trust Center — *Disciplined Entrepreneurship*
- **URL:** https://www.d-eship.com/step2/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary (author's own site) · **Depth:** FULL · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** Before anything else, narrow to one homogeneous beachhead market and dominate it.
- **Framework:** Three conditions for a homogeneous market — (a) end users all use *the same product*, (b) the *sales process is the same* (interchangeable language, value proposition, channel), (c) *strong word of mouth exists between end users*. Plus eight evaluation criteria: is the customer well-funded; are they accessible to your sales force; do they have a compelling reason to buy; can you deliver a whole product; is there entrenched competition; can you leverage this segment into others; is it consistent with the team's values and passions; how quickly can you win it.
- **Relevance:** The core "is this segment actually a segment?" test, and eight of the scoring-matrix dimensions.
- **Limitations:** Written for venture-scale tech startups; "well-funded customer" needs softening for local business and expert products.

### 1.2 Market Types — Existing / Resegmented / New / Clone
- **Author / org:** Steve Blank — *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*; Customer Development Manifesto
- **URL:** https://steveblank.com/2009/09/10/customer-development-manifesto-part-4/ · https://steveblank.com/category/market-types/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** Market type determines everything downstream — spend, messaging, and how fast adoption can possibly go.
- **Framework:** Existing market (customers can name the market and the features they want) · Resegmented market (cheaper, or a niche existing suppliers failed to serve) · New market (nobody could do this before, because of cost / availability / skill) · Clone market (a proven model transplanted to a new geography).
- **Relevance:** A segment's *market type* changes which channel and which validation experiment is even valid. A new-market segment cannot be validated with keyword volume.
- **Limitations:** Snippet-level review; the full taxonomy detail is in the book.

### 1.3 Three Tiers of Noncustomers
- **Author / org:** W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne — *Blue Ocean Strategy*
- **URL:** https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/three-tiers-of-noncustomers/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary (authors' institute) · **Depth:** FULL · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** The biggest audience opportunity is usually outside the current customer base, not inside it.
- **Framework:** Tier 1 "soon-to-be" (buy minimally out of necessity, mentally already gone) · Tier 2 "refusing" (evaluated the category and consciously said no — unacceptable, unaffordable, too complicated) · Tier 3 "unexplored" (never considered the category at all; sit in a different market). Method: look for *commonalities across the tiers*, not differences.
- **Relevance:** The single best generator of **unexpected audiences**. Tier 2 refusers are where "expensive pain, low marketing saturation" segments hide.
- **Limitations:** Corporate-strategy origin; needs translating into small-business language.

### 1.4 Market attractiveness / segment prioritisation matrices
- **Author / org:** Umbrex (consulting framework library); Malcolm McDonald; Notion Capital MPM
- **URL:** https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/segmentation-attractiveness-fit-matrix/ · https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/targeting-prioritization-matrix-size-growth-profitability-strategic-fit/ · https://www.notioncapital.com/resources/introducing-the-market-prioritisation-matrix-mpm
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework library · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4
- **Main idea:** Score segments on two axes — market attractiveness and ability to win — using 5–10 explicitly weighted criteria per axis, each with a written rubric for what a "1" and a "5" mean.
- **Framework:** Attractiveness = size, growth, profit potential / WTP, competitive intensity, pricing dynamics, regulatory risk. Fit = capabilities, product fit, brand relevance, channel access, cost-to-serve, time to credible scale. Weights sum to 100% per axis. Revisit quarterly against actuals (win rates, margin, retention).
- **Relevance:** Direct parent of `../templates/scoring-matrix-template.md` — especially the rule that **every scale point needs a written anchor**, or scoring is vibes with decimals.
- **Limitations:** Generic consulting framing; nothing on solo or micro businesses.
- **⚠ Superseded in part:** McDonald appears here only as a passing citation. **His method is now documented properly in §10.1**, read from his own full-length paper. Use that entry for anything concerning market definition, market mapping, micro-segments or the segment checklist.

### 1.5 Market > Offer > Persuasion; the four market criteria
- **Author / org:** Alex Hormozi — *$100M Offers*
- **URL:** https://theprocesshacker.com/blog/100m-offers-alex-hormozi-summary · https://thepowermoves.com/100-million-offers-summary-review/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book, read via secondary summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4 (summaries of a primary work)
- **Main idea:** "Sell to a starving crowd." A great offer aimed at a bad market loses to a mediocre offer aimed at a great one.
- **Framework:** Four market criteria — **massive pain · purchasing power · easy to target · growing**. Hierarchy: Market > Offer > Persuasion.
- **Relevance:** The most compressed usable definition of a good audience. "Easy to target" (do they belong to identifiable associations, lists, groups, channels?) is the criterion most often skipped, and it is load-bearing for free acquisition.
- **Limitations:** Info-marketing context; "growing" is often unverifiable for niche B2B. Read via summaries, not the primary text, in this pass.

### 1.6 Underserved / niche market discovery
- **Author / org:** Entrepreneur; Invoke Media; Mailchimp Resources; The Strategy Institute
- **URL:** https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/why-focusing-on-underserved-or-niche-markets-can-be-a/396218 · https://invokemedia.co.uk/identifying-underserved-market-segments-unlock-hidden-growth-opportunities · https://mailchimp.com/resources/untapped-market/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Practitioner articles · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 3
- **Main idea:** An underserved segment is not necessarily small — it is *overlooked*. Find the intersection of your unique strengths and their unmet needs.
- **Framework:** Market gap analysis (what exists vs. what is wanted); 80/20 on the existing base; viability check (big enough to pay, not so contested you cannot win).
- **Limitations:** Generic; no repeatable detection procedure. Used for vocabulary and structure, not for claims.

### 1.7 Bottom-up market sizing (TAM / SAM / SOM)
- **Author / org:** Forum VC; Qubit Capital; HubSpot
- **URL:** https://www.forumvc.com/thought-pieces/understand-and-define-your-market-size · https://qubit.capital/blog/bottom-up-market-sizing · https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/tam-sam-som
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Practitioner / VC guidance · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4
- **Main idea:** Build the number from countable customers × realistic price, not from a research-report headline.
- **Framework:** Market size = ACV × number of reachable customers. Cross-check bottom-up against top-down; landing within ~2× suggests sane assumptions.
- **Relevance:** A segment that cannot be counted cannot be targeted. "How many of them are there, and where is the list?" is a reachability test disguised as a sizing test.

## Cluster 2 — Jobs To Be Done & demand-side research

### 2.1 Marketing Malpractice / the milkshake
- **Author / org:** Clayton Christensen, Scott Cook, Taddy Hall — HBR, 2005
- **URL:** https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/clay-christensens-milkshake-marketing
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Academic / practitioner article, read via HBS Working Knowledge · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 (originating work)
- **Main idea:** Segment around the **job the customer hires the product to do**, not around who the customer is. The morning-commute milkshake and the afternoon-treat milkshake are two different markets bought by the same demographic.
- **Relevance:** The core argument for why a demographic audience definition is usually a non-answer, and why one product can have several genuinely distinct audiences.
- **Limitations:** Strategyn (Ulwick) publicly argues the milkshake example itself violates rigorous JTBD segmentation. Present it as an illustration, not as a method.

### 2.2 Outcome-Driven Innovation; the Opportunity Algorithm; underserved outcomes
- **Author / org:** Anthony (Tony) Ulwick / Strategyn
- **URL:** https://www.marketingjournal.org/the-path-to-growth-the-opportunity-algorithm-anthony-ulwick/ · https://strategyn.com/market-segmentation-is-soured-by-milkshake-marketing/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcome-Driven_Innovation
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary author + encyclopaedic · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 / 4
- **Main idea:** Segment by **unmet outcome**, not by demographics or behaviour. Opportunity = importance + max(importance − satisfaction, 0).
- **Framework:** Scores run 0–20. Above ~15 = severely underserved (build and target here). Below ~10 = overserved (disruptable from below with cheaper and simpler).
- **Relevance:** Gives a *numeric* definition of "underserved audience" and — critically — of **overserved** audiences, which is where budget and low-cost segments come from.
- **Limitations:** Requires quantitative outcome surveys most small businesses will never run. Use the logic (importance vs. satisfaction gap) qualitatively.

### 2.3 The Four Forces of Progress & the Switch Interview
- **Author / org:** Bob Moesta & Chris Spiek (The Re-Wired Group); jobstobedone.org; Alan Klement
- **URL:** https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/ · https://jtbd.info/the-forces-of-progress-4408bf995153 · https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/bob-moesta-on-unpacking-customer-motivations-with-jobs-to-be-done/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary-adjacent · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** "A struggling moment causes demand" — not the product. People switch only when **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit**.
- **Framework:** Push (pain of the current situation) · Pull (attraction of the new) · Anxiety (fear of the new) · Habit (attachment to the current mess). The Switch Interview reconstructs the timeline backwards from purchase to first thought.
- **Relevance:** Converts "urgency" from an adjective into four assessable forces, and tells you in advance what an audience's objections will be — they *are* the Anxiety and Habit forces.
- **Practical note from the same cluster:** roughly 10 recent-buyer interviews of 30–60 minutes typically surfaces the 3–5 struggling moments covering most of a market.

### 2.4 The Mom Test — asking about past behaviour
- **Author / org:** Rob Fitzpatrick — *The Mom Test* (RU: «Спроси маму»)
- **URL:** https://kokoc.com/blog/custdev-chto-eto-primery/ · https://skillbox.ru/media/marketing/kak-proverit-biznesideyu-s-pomoshchyu-custdev/
- **Language:** EN book, read via RU secondary sources · **Type:** Book · **Depth:** SNIPPET (secondary) · **Reliability:** 5 (the work) / 3 (these summaries)
- **Main idea:** Never ask opinions or predictions. Ask what has already happened: what they did, when, what it cost them, what they tried, what they paid for.
- **Relevance:** The single rule that stops audience research from producing polite lies. Encoded as a hard rule in the skill.

### 2.5 Lead users & extreme users
- **Author / org:** Eric von Hippel (MIT) — lead-user theory, 1986; HBR "Let the Users Take the Lead"
- **URL:** https://hbr.org/2008/02/let-the-users-take-the-lead · https://www.europeanbusinessreview.com/extreme-users-understanding-their-role-in-innovation-and-design/ · https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-user-innovation-revolution
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Academic + practitioner · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 / 3
- **Main idea:** Users at the leading edge experience mainstream needs *early and in extreme form*, and they build their own workarounds.
- **Evidence carried in the corpus:** in studies of "extreme" sports clubs, more than a third of members had built or modified equipment for their own use; lead-user-derived concepts are reported at multi-fold higher sales potential than internally generated ones.
- **Relevance:** The strongest single heuristic for finding unexpected audiences — **look for who is already hacking together a solution.** Also covers *analogous markets*: people in another industry facing the same problem in harsher form.
- **Limitations:** Secondary sources for the numeric claims; treat magnitudes as directional.

## Cluster 3 — Positioning from the audience

### 3.1 Positioning components; best-fit customers
- **Author / org:** April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome*; "A Quickstart Guide to Positioning"
- **URL:** https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning · https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/summary-april-dunford-on-product
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary · **Depth:** FULL (quickstart) + SNIPPET (Lenny summary) · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** "Positioning defines how your product is a leader at delivering something that a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about."
- **Framework (the order matters):** competitive alternatives → unique attributes → differentiated value → **best-fit customers (who cares a lot about that value)** → market category.
- **Relevance:** Establishes the causal direction the skill enforces: **you do not pick an audience and then invent value; you find your value and then find who cares most about it.** Also supplies the "phantom competitor" warning — teams list alternatives they never actually lose to.
- **Limitations:** B2B-tech-centric; the category step needs adaptation for local services and info products.

### 3.2 Five stages of awareness; market sophistication
- **Author / org:** Eugene Schwartz — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966)
- **URL:** https://copyposse.com/blog/5-levels-of-market-awareness-how-to-speak-to-your-target-audience-part-1/ · https://www.activecampaign.com/blog/marketing-stages-awareness-and-buyer-cycle · https://nordiccopy.com/market-sophistication/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book, via practitioner summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 (the work) / 3 (summaries)
- **Main idea:** Unaware → Problem-aware → Solution-aware → Product-aware → Most aware. Meet the prospect at their level; and account for how many competing claims the market has already heard (sophistication stages 1–5).
- **Relevance:** Converts "audience" into an *addressable state*. Two segments with identical demographics but different awareness levels need different channels, different content and different acquisition economics. Unaware audiences are the most expensive to acquire and the worst fit for free channels.

### 3.3 Buyer personas & the Five Rings of Buying Insight
- **Author / org:** Adele Revella — Buyer Persona Institute
- **URL:** https://www.buyerpersona.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Buyer-Persona-Manifesto-2016.pdf · https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/mistakes-buyer-personas/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary (manifesto PDF) · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4
- **Main idea:** A persona is only useful if it captures the **buying decision**, not the biography.
- **Framework — Five Rings:** Priority Initiative (what triggered the search) · Success Factors (outcomes expected) · Perceived Barriers (why they might not buy you) · Buyer's Journey (who is involved, what they consult) · Decision Criteria (what they compare).
- **Relevance:** The corrective to persona theatre ("Marketing Marina, 34, loves yoga"). The skill's segment template is built from the five rings, not from demographics.

### 3.4 Mass reach, light buyers & the limits of targeting
- **Author / org:** Byron Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass Institute — *How Brands Grow*
- **URL:** https://dovetail.com/market-research/how-brands-grow-changed-market-research/ · https://learn.zappi.io/article/336-principles-of-how-brands-grow · https://www.themarketingstudent.com/how-brands-grow/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Research-based book, via summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 (the work) / 3 (summaries)
- **Main idea:** In established consumer categories, brands grow mainly by **reaching more category buyers**, especially light buyers, not by deepening loyalty in a narrow segment. Competing brands' customer bases look statistically alike.
- **Relevance:** The most important **counterweight** in this corpus. It bounds the whole discipline: sharp segmentation is decisive for a startup, a service business, or a challenger with no distribution; it is much weaker advice for an established mass-market brand. The skill is required to state which regime it is operating in.
- **Limitations:** Evidence base is strongest in repertoire FMCG categories; contested for considered and B2B purchases. Some practitioners argue it over-simplifies. Carry the tension; do not resolve it dishonestly.

## Cluster 4 — Product-market fit measurement & segmentation-by-fit

### 4.1 The Product-Market Fit Engine
- **Author / org:** Rahul Vohra (Superhuman), building on Sean Ellis's survey — First Round Review
- **URL:** https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/ · https://blog.superhuman.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Case study, primary author · **Depth:** FULL · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** PMF is measurable, and **segmentation is the lever that moves it**.
- **Framework — the four survey questions** (asked of users who used the product at least twice in two weeks): (1) How would you feel if you could no longer use X? *very / somewhat / not disappointed*; (2) What type of people do you think would most benefit from X?; (3) What is the main benefit you receive from X?; (4) How can we improve X for you?
  **Benchmark:** ~40% "very disappointed" (Sean Ellis, across roughly 100 startups).
  **The key move:** Superhuman scored 22%. Grouping responses by persona and filtering to the persona that dominated the "very disappointed" group raised the score to **32% with no product change at all** — then to 58% over three quarters. Build the **High-Expectation Customer** profile from that group. Ignore "not disappointed" entirely. Mine "somewhat disappointed who already value the core benefit" for the roadmap.
- **Relevance:** Proof that *who you count as your audience* is itself a growth lever, plus a ready-made procedure for any business that already has customers. Question 2 ("what type of people would most benefit") is a free unexpected-audience generator.
- **Limitations:** Needs an existing engaged user base; useless pre-launch.

### 4.2 ABCDX segmentation
- **Author / org:** Ilya Krasinsky (RU startup ecosystem); documented by Kontur, Ivan Zamesin, Product Focus, sostav.ru
- **URL:** https://kontur.ru/compass/spravka-compass/48509-abcdxsegmentaciya_klientov_i_celevoj_auditorii · https://vc.ru/marketing/206489-abcdx-segmentaciya · https://zamesin.ru/books/product-howto/abcdx-segmentation/ *(BLOCKED)* · https://blog.productfocus.ru/abcdx-segmentation/ *(BLOCKED)*
- **Language:** RU · **Type:** Framework · **Depth:** FULL (Kontur) + BLOCKED (Zamesin, Product Focus) · **Reliability:** 4
- **Main idea:** Rank paying segments by **how much value they get × how fast they pay**, not by industry or size.
- **Framework:** **A** — acute problem, buys fast, minimal objections, extracts maximum value. **B** — has the problem often but is not actively solving it; needs persuasion, trials, special offers. **C** — recent or mild problem, gets indirect value, haggles, delays payment. **D** — no real need; even if they buy they get no value and generate negative experience. **X** — would be an A-grade account but needs product work to serve (large corporates, government); treat as a roadmap signal, not as current demand.
  Attributes split into **formalized** (public: decision-maker role, industry classification code, headcount, open vacancies, licences, certifications) and **non-formalized** (only surfaced in conversation: circumstances of the problem, monetary damage, deadline, budget).
  Operational moves: encode segment markers into sales scripts; work A and B intensively while automating C and D; tie sales KPIs to A/B leads.
- **Relevance:** The best RU-native complement to ICP work, and the sharpest available articulation of a **D-segment** — an audience you should refuse. Feeds the anti-audience concept and the red-flags checklist directly.
- **Limitations:** The Kontur article attributes discussion to Andrey Tomachinsky and does not name the originator; attribution to Krasinsky comes from other RU sources in the corpus. Requires existing paying customers.

### 4.3 Product-Channel Fit / Four Fits
- **Author / org:** Brian Balfour (Reforge; ex-VP Growth, HubSpot)
- **URL:** https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework · https://brianbalfour.com/essays/product-channel-fit-for-growth
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Framework, primary · **Depth:** FULL (four fits) + SNIPPET (product-channel essay) · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** Four interdependent fits — Market↔Product, Product↔Channel, Channel↔Model, Model↔Market. Fail one and growth stalls regardless of the others.
- **Key rule:** **"Products are built to fit with channels. Channels do not mold to products."** You do not set the channel's rules — Google, Meta, Gmail and the app stores do.
- **Model-market arithmetic:** ARPU × total market customers × capture % ≥ target revenue. Channel-model fit runs on the ARPU↔CAC spectrum: low ARPU cannot fund high-touch channels.
- **Relevance:** The reason the skill scores **audience-channel fit** rather than just "audience quality." A segment that is perfect on pain and money but unreachable by any channel your model can afford is not a viable audience.

## Cluster 5 — Channels, habitats & acquisition

### 5.1 Bullseye Framework & 19 traction channels
- **Author / org:** Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares — *Traction*
- **URL:** https://medium.com/@yegg/the-bullseye-framework-for-getting-traction-ef49d05bfd7e *(BLOCKED — 403)* · https://growthmethod.com/bullseye-framework/ · https://growthmethod.com/traction-channels/ · https://www.designwithvalue.com/bullseye-framework
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book + practitioner summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET (the author's own Medium post returned 403) · **Reliability:** 5 (the work) / 4 (summaries)
- **Main idea:** Outer ring — brainstorm all 19 channels and one cheap idea for each. Middle ring — run 3–4 cheap tests in parallel. Inner ring — when one works, pour everything into it until it stops scaling.
- **Scale of a test:** the corpus repeatedly cites roughly a month and under ~$1,000 to pick a channel — i.e. **channel choice is an experiment, not a debate.**
- **Relevance:** Supplies the channel list and, more importantly, the discipline that channel selection is decided by cheap parallel tests rather than opinion.

### 5.2 Audience intelligence / watering holes
- **Author / org:** Rand Fishkin — SparkToro
- **URL:** https://sparktoro.com/blog/ · https://stratabeat.com/sparktoro-rand-fishkin-interview/ · https://beomniscient.com/blog/rand-fishkin-sparktoro/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Tool + practitioner interviews · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4 (2 for tool-capability claims)
- **Main idea:** Find the specific publications, podcasts, YouTube channels, subreddits and accounts a defined audience actually consumes — then go there instead of buying broad ads.
- **Quoted method:** learn who the audience's sources of influence are, then "swim in that water" long enough to speak their language and make references they recognise.
- **Relevance:** The operational definition of a **habitat**, and the reason the skill's output must name specific places rather than platform categories. "Be on LinkedIn" is not an answer; "these six LinkedIn creators and these three Slack communities" is.
- **Limitations:** Vendor-adjacent. Coverage is weak for RU/CIS platforms — RU habitat work needs TGStat and VK-native tooling instead.

### 5.3 Reddit / Discord / community-led growth
- **Author / org:** Various practitioner sources; GummySearch product documentation
- **URL:** https://getathenic.com/blog/community-led-growth-reddit-discord-forums-revenue · https://www.singlegrain.com/social-media-management/best-practices/reddit-communities-for-your-niche/ · https://gummysearch.com/product/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Practitioner + tool · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 3 (2 for tool claims)
- **Main idea:** Give value, build trust, mention the product only when genuinely relevant. Reddit threads double as a real-time keyword and pain-point source: the questions people repeatedly ask *are* the content plan.
- **Useful taxonomy (from GummySearch's product framing):** conversation types worth isolating — **Pain Points · Solution Requests · Money Talk · Hot Discussions**. Reusable as an analytical lens with or without the tool.
- **⚠ Currency warning:** search results in this pass indicate **GummySearch is shutting down**. Do not present it as a live recommendation without checking. Alternatives exist but were not verified here.

### 5.4 Meta Ad Library as a research surface
- **Author / org:** Meta (the library itself); agency practitioner guides
- **URL:** https://midsummer.agency/blog/meta-ads-library/ · https://commitagency.com/insights/using-the-meta-ad-library-for-competitor-research-and-creative-inspiration/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Practitioner guides · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 3
- **Main idea:** A free, public, searchable database of active ads. Sort by start date: **an ad running 60–90+ days is almost certainly profitable**, because nobody keeps paying for losers.
- **Relevance:** The cheapest way to read a competitor's *audience hypotheses* and message angles at zero cost, and a proxy that a segment has workable paid economics.
- **Limitations:** Longevity is a proxy, not proof. Meta-only; RU markets need VK/Yandex equivalents and ad-spy services.

### 5.5 Intent & hiring signals for B2B audiences
- **Author / org:** Landbase, Bombora, Demandbase, HeyReach (vendor content)
- **URL:** https://www.landbase.com/blog/hiring-signals-icp-roles-list-building · https://bombora.com/core-concepts/how-to-identify-and-engage-your-b2b-target-audience/ · https://www.heyreach.io/blog/outbound-icp-signals
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Vendor content marketing · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 2
- **Main idea:** Job postings, funding rounds, tech-stack changes and executive job-changes are public, timestamped admissions of a problem.
- **Claim carried in the corpus (vendor-sourced, directional only):** new executives are several times more likely to start a technology evaluation in their first year; re-engaging a former champion at their new company converts far better than cold outbound.
- **Relevance:** Turns "trigger events" from a concept into a findable list. Hiring for a role means budget was approved for that problem.
- **Limitations:** All of these sell data products. The *mechanism* is sound and independently checkable; the conversion percentages are not verified here.

### 5.6 Do Things That Don't Scale
- **Author / org:** Paul Graham (Y Combinator), 2013

### 7.1 Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter
- **Author / org:** Peter van Westendorp (1970s); documented by OpinionX, SurveyMonkey, Conjointly, Umbrex
- **URL:** https://www.opinionx.co/blog/van-westendorp-pricing-guide · https://www.surveymonkey.com/market-research/resources/van-westendorp-price-sensitivity-meter/ · https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/pricing-frameworks/van-westendorp-price-sensitivity-meter/

### 9.1 Competing Against Luck — **the primary source, read in full**
- **Author / org:** Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David S. Duncan (2016). Companion to the HBR article "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done" (Sept 2016) and to "Marketing Malpractice" (HBR, Dec 2005, with Scott Cook).
- **Source:** full text supplied by the user, `D:\книги\позиционирование\Competing Against Luck_ ... .md` (2,658 lines). Read directly.
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book, primary author · **Depth:** **FULL** · **Reliability:** 5
- **Supersedes:** the previous snippet-level entry, and **resolves the paywall caveat** recorded in the earlier coverage self-assessment.

**The definition:** a job is **"the progress that a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance."** *Progress* denotes movement toward a goal — a process, rarely a discrete event. *Circumstance* is intrinsic: a job can only be defined relative to the specific context in which it arises.

**What this source contributes that no secondary source in the corpus did:**

1. **The abstraction test.** *"If the architecture of the system or product can only be met by products within the same product class, the concept of the Job to Be Done does not apply."* List the candidates; if they all come from one category you have a need, a preference or a spec. Worked examples: the 12-oz milkshake (not a job) vs. "keep me occupied on the drive and full until 10 a.m." (banana, doughnut, bagel, Snickers, coffee → a job); the Tyvek technical spec (not a job) vs. "keep my family warm in a Boston winter" (blown pulp, fiberglass, Tyvek, sweaters, dehumidifiers, **or move to San Francisco**).
2. **The verbs-and-nouns test.** *"If you or a colleague describes a Job to Be Done in adjectives and adverbs, it is not a valid job."* **"Convenience" is not a job.**
3. **Need ≠ Job ≠ Life theme.** Needs are too generic to direct anything ("I need to eat"). Life themes are too abstract to trigger a purchase — **"Helping me feel like a good dad is not a Job to Be Done."** Jobs overcome the obstacles blocking life themes, in specific circumstances.
4. **Big Hire vs. Little Hire.** The purchase vs. actual consumption. *"The Big Hire might suggest that a product has solved a customer's job, but only a consistent series of Little Hires can confirm it."* Most data tracks only the former.
5. **"What has to get fired for my product to get hired?"** Something is always fired, including doing nothing. *"Companies don't think about this enough."*
6. **The five job-hunting grounds:** jobs in your own life · **nonconsumption** · workarounds and compensating behaviours · **negative jobs** (*"In my experience, negative jobs are often the best innovation opportunities"*) · unusual uses.
7. **The Three Fallacies of Innovation Data:** active vs. passive data · surface growth · conforming data. Plus the underlying warning — *"All data is man-made"*, *"Data is not the phenomena"*, and Tom Monahan's **Museum of False Precision**.
8. **Cluster, don't segment.** *"Understanding jobs is about clustering insights into a coherent picture, rather than segmenting down to finer and finer slices."* Deconstructing episodes into binary bits *"destroys meaning in the process."* Jobs insights are *"more like stories than statistics."*
9. **Depth over breadth in sampling.** *"A handful of data points from a huge sample"* is the standard mistake; what is needed is *"a huge number of data points from a smaller sample size."*
10. **Why job-fit commands a premium.** The full cost of a failing solution — wasted time, frustration, money on poor alternatives — is already being paid, so a premium price *"looks small compared with what you've already been spending."* Distinguished sharply from **lock-in premium** (printer ink), which *causes* anxiety and which customers resent.
11. **The explicit boundaries of the theory** (see below).
12. **"Signal 'this product is not for you.'"** Direct authorial support for the Anti-ICP, framed as defect prevention rather than positioning.

**Cases carrying quantitative outcomes** (all as reported by the authors): the Detroit condo developer — **raised price $3,500 profitably, grew 25% in 2007 while the market fell 49%**, after discovering the blocker was the dining room table, not a feature · SNHU online — ~$535m revenue, 34% CAGR over six years · Kimberly-Clark Depend Silhouette — $60m year one, 30% growth year two, **no cannibalisation** · Airbnb — ~40% of guests would not have travelled at all · Arm & Hammer — the original baking-soda box now **under 7%** of consumer revenue · Pampers China — ~$1.6bn by 2013 · QuickBooks — half the functionality at twice the price, global leader · American Girl — sold to Mattel for $700m after **zero** upfront research and one focus group whose verdict was wrong.

**The theory's stated limits, in the authors' own words:** Jobs Theory is not useful where there is **no real struggle** or existing solutions are **good enough**; not useful where the decision is essentially mathematical (commodities trading); and **"cost or efficiency is not a core element of a job."** Christensen also draws the ethical line explicitly — the theory explains why customers choose, *not* whether you should offer the solution (his own example: cigarettes). And on anomalies: *"Anomalies do not disprove anything"* — they mark a boundary to be defined, not a reason to discard the tool.

**Corroborating detail retained from the earlier entry:** Christensen's demonstration that his own attributes (64, 6'8", size 16, Honda minivan, suburban Boston) *"have not yet caused me to go out and buy the New York Times today"* — correlation is not causation, and customer characteristics are correlation. Plus the Air Force cockpit story (via Todd Rose, *The End of Average*): remeasuring for the average pilot produced a cockpit that fit **none** of them. *"Innovating toward 'average' is doomed to fail."*

**Limitations of this source, stated fairly:** it is a single-author-team account written to advocate a theory, and the cases are retold by the authors rather than independently audited. The financial outcomes above should be read as reported figures, not verified ones. Christensen concedes the retrofit risk himself — *"It is always tempting to look at innovation success stories and retrofit the explanation."* Note also that Strategyn (Ulwick, §2.2) publicly disputes the milkshake as a rigorous segmentation exemplar; that tension is carried in the report rather than resolved.

### 9.3 Jobs to Be Done Theory
- **Author / org:** Clayton Christensen Institute
- **URL:** https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Institutional reference · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5
- **Main idea:** people do not simply buy products; they **hire** them to make progress in specific circumstances. Theory developed by Christensen with long-time collaborator **Bob Moesta**.
- **Framework:** the three dimensions — **functional** (the practical task), **emotional** (what they want to feel or avoid feeling), **social** (how they want to be seen by others). Nailing all three sharply increases the odds of success.
- **Relevance:** §3 of the JTBD engine. The social dimension is the one that decides B2B deals and is absent from every requirements document.

### 9.4 Demand-Side Sales 101
- **Author / org:** Bob Moesta (with Greg Engle) — book; Business of Software talk
- **URL:** https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/demand-side-sales-101-bob-moesta-online/ · https://scottburleson.substack.com/p/book-summary-demand-side-sales-101 · https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2020/book-notes-demand-side-sales/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book, via author talk + summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4 (summaries of a primary work)
- **Main idea:** two forces push a decision forward — the **struggling moment** and the **pull of the new** — and two block it: the **habit of the present** and the **anxiety of the new**. **Push + Pull must exceed Anxiety + Habit before a purchase happens.**
- **Canonical illustration:** the push of sleeping badly · the pull of a friend raving about a new mattress · the anxiety of "will a new mattress even help?" · the habit of "my mattress is bad but I'm used to it."
- **Relevance:** the four-forces diagnostic in `../knowledge/jtbd-engine.md` §4, and the reason the skill asks *which force is binding* before recommending any message. Most budget is spent amplifying Pull when the constraint is Anxiety or Habit.

### 9.11 Monetizing Innovation
- **Author / org:** Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke (Simon-Kucher)

### 9.12 Economic Value Estimation
- **Author / org:** Thomas Nagle et al. — *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing*
- **URL:** https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-strategy-and/9781000915969/xhtml/C_004_c2.xhtml · https://www.ibbaka.com/ibbaka-market-blog/what-makes-a-good-value-model-tom-nagles-view
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Standard textbook · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 / 4
- **Framework:** **Total economic value = Reference value + Differentiation value**, where reference value is the price of the customer's **best alternative** and differentiation value is the worth of what distinguishes your offering from it. ### 9.13 WTP research methods compared
- **Author / org:** Conjointly; Umbrex; OpinionX; Quali-Fi; Drive Research
- **URL:** https://conjointly.com/blog/gabor-granger-or-van-westendorp/ · https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/pricing-frameworks/gabor-granger-method/ · https://www.opinionx.co/blog/gabor-granger-method · https://quali-fi.com/learn/pricing-research
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Method comparison, practitioner + tool vendors · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4
- **Main idea:** **Van Westendorp** measures a perceived acceptable price *range* and suits **new or innovative** offers where no reference point exists. **Gabor-Granger** tests purchase likelihood at defined price points and produces a **demand curve**, suiting **established** categories. **Conjoint / discrete choice** measures feature-price trade-offs and simulates competitive scenarios — most powerful, most expensive, most complex.
- **The practical guidance adopted:** Van Westendorp + Gabor-Granger together give roughly **80% of the insight at 30% of the cost** of conjoint; reserve conjoint for when price and features must be optimised jointly.
- **Relevance:** the method-selection table in `../knowledge/willingness-to-pay.md` §5, and the hard rule that **all stated-preference methods cap at `L3`** regardless of sample size.

### 10.1 Market Segmentation: How to Do It and How to Profit from It
- **Author / org:** Malcolm McDonald (Emeritus Professor, Cranfield School of Management) & Ian Dunbar (co-founder, The Market Segmentation Company). 4th ed., Wiley 2012; earlier eds. Butterworth-Heinemann.
- **Read via:** McDonald's own summary paper *"Market Segmentation"* — https://malcolm-mcdonald.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/malcolm-mcdonald-international-market-segmentation.pdf — **retrieved and extracted in full (951 lines of text via `pdftotext`)**. Supporting pages: https://malcolm-mcdonald.com/market-segmentation-and-market-mapping/ (FULL) · https://malcolm-mcdonald.com/market-segmentation-foundation-framework-future/ (FULL) · https://www.marketingjournal.org/market-segmentation-still-the-bedrock-of-commercial-success-malcolm-mcdonald/ (FULL)
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Book, via the author's own full-length summary paper · **Depth:** **FULL** (the paper) / the book itself not read · **Reliability:** 5

**The market definition rule:** *"A market is the aggregation of all products or services which customers regard as being capable of satisfying the same need."*

**⚠ Note the corroboration — this matters for the skill's own evidence rules.** This is functionally identical to Christensen's abstraction test (§9.1), reached independently from the market-structure tradition rather than the demand-side one. Two independent traditions converging on the same boundary test raises that rule from "one authority's assertion" to a corroborated principle. It is the strongest cross-source agreement in this corpus.

**What this source contributes that nothing else in the corpus did:**

1. **The market map with quantified leverage.** Plot the distribution and value-added chain from suppliers to final users; list company types at each junction; quantify each; then, starting at the final-user junction, record **the volume/value decided at each junction**. Worked example: final users 300k / 70% decided there · contractors 75k / 20% · retailers 180k / 10% → **"30 per cent of annual sales are decided at junctions other than the final user junction."** Rule: *"Segmentation can and should be carried out at all major junctions on the market map, not just at the final user junction."* Guesstimating is explicitly endorsed on a first pass, with the gap logged as follow-up.
2. **Micro-segments as the bottom-up engine.** Catalogue *actual purchase combinations* — What is bought / Where / When / How / Who / Why (benefits sought) / size. **30–50 micro-segments is normal; 7–10 final segments is the practical ceiling** (*"impracticable to deal with more than between 7 or 10"*).
3. **KDF vs CPI.** **Key Discriminating Features** are only those features customers actually use to choose between alternatives (table stakes excluded). **Critical Purchase Influences** are the *benefits* those KDFs deliver — *"for some customers it is only by combining certain KDFs that they attain the benefit(s) they seek."*
4. **The 100-point constant-sum allocation.** CPI importance per micro-segment is set *"by distributing 100 points between the CPIs"* — which is what makes similarity measurable and clustering possible.
5. **The Step-7 segment checklist**, whose fourth question tests the organisation rather than the segmentation: *"Will the company change and adopt a segment focus?"*
6. **The commoditisation diagnostic.** Mature markets grow at the low-price end; the top end is immune; **the middle suffers.** But *"competing only on price is to assume that this is the main requirement of customers, whereas the truth is that this is rarely the case"* — a lack of segmentation understanding *"forces them to trade on price, so encouraging the market to become a commodity market."* Reaching commodity status is *"by no means inevitable."*
7. **Segment ≠ sector**, and *a priori* segmentation as *"the gravest mistake of all."*

**Aphorisms carried into the skill:** *"Anyone who says 'we segment markets by ....' is totally missing the point"* · *"You don't impose segmentation — you uncover it"* · *"Positioning is easy. Market segmentation is difficult. **Positioning problems stem from poor segmentation**"* · *"Select a segment and serve it. Do not straddle segments and sit between them"* · *"Boy George and the Archbishop of Canterbury are both As, but they don't behave the same"* · *"There is no such thing as an average customer — it's a bit like saying my head is in the oven and my feet are in the fridge, so on average I am quite comfortable"* · *"Segment or die."*

**Cases, as reported by the author:** **ICI Fertilisers** — the industry, *"especially the sales force,"* believed 100% of farmers bought on price; segmentation found **seven farmer types**, of which the genuinely price-driven "Arthur" was **only 10% of the market**. "Oliver" ran satellite-linked soil analysis to maximise yield and bought ingredients cheaply *only because no supplier had understood his needs*; "David" wanted crops and livestock to look healthy. ICI became *"the most profitable subsidiary of ICI and the only profitable fertilizer company in the country."* · **A photocopier incumbent** with 80% share and massive margins ignored a Japanese entrant, cut prices in response, and **fell to 10% share within three years** by competing in all segments with one product. · **P&G / Wal-Mart** as the positive channel case — demand pull at the consumer end, till-triggered invoicing and replenishment at the customer end.

**Statistics cited by McDonald (secondary attributions, not his own data unless noted):**
- **His own 35-year analysis of 3,000 marketing plans: only 300 contained proper needs-based segmentation — 90% did not.** This is his primary finding and the most defensible figure here.
- Christensen (HBR): of 30,000 new products launched in the USA, **85% failed because of poor market segmentation.** ⚠ McDonald dates this to 2006 in the text but his reference list points at "Marketing Malpractice" (2005). **Attribution is inconsistent in the source itself — cite with care, or not at all.**
- Rigby et al. (HBR 2002) on CRM: trying to implement it without segmentation is *"like trying to build a house without engineering measures or an architecture plan."*
- PIMS data relating relative market share and relative product quality to ROI.

**Limitations, stated fairly:**
- **The book itself was not read** — only the author's own full-length summary paper. The paper is dense and self-contained on method, but the book's worked templates, checklists and statistical detail are not represented here. If a client engagement turns on the exact clustering procedure, buy the book.
- The paper is **self-published advocacy** by the method's author and co-founder of a consultancy selling it. The method is sound and widely taught (it sits in the CIM syllabus), but the case outcomes are the author's retellings.
- **OCR corruption:** the extracted text reads *"between 30 and so micro-segments"*. Read in context as **30–50**; flagged in `../knowledge/market-mapping.md` §4 rather than silently corrected.
- **The method is heavyweight.** McDonald concedes it is *"hard work, and is time-consuming."* It presumes sales data, volumes and often commissioned research — resources most users of this skill do not have. Positioned in the skill as the rigorous tier, not the default.
- The segment-attractiveness / Directional Policy Matrix material referenced on McDonald's site sits behind a slide-deck download that was **not obtained**; the attractiveness scoring in this skill continues to rest on §1.4 and the TAS design.

## Cluster 12 — Pain: root-cause discovery and the ethics of pain-based messaging

### 12.1 The Extended Parallel Process Model — fear appeals
- **Author / org:** Kim Witte (communications scholar); Witte & Allen (2000) meta-analysis
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_parallel_process_model · https://ua.pressbooks.pub/persuasiontheoryinaction/chapter/fear-appeals-the-extended-parallel-process-model/ · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1090198111418108 · https://regulatorwatch.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Witte-and-Allen-2000-A-Meta-Analysis-of-Fear-Appeals-Implications-for-.pdf
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Academic model + meta-analysis, via encyclopaedic and OER summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 5 (the model) / 4 (these summaries)

**The mechanism.** A recipient of a threat message runs two appraisals: **threat** (severity × susceptibility) and **efficacy** (response efficacy × self-efficacy).

- **High threat + high efficacy → danger control.** The person acts on the problem; the message is accepted.
- **High threat + LOW efficacy → fear control.** The person acts on the *fear* — **defensive avoidance, denial, reactance.** The message is rejected.

> *"Most fear campaigns fail not because fear is the wrong lever but because they crank threat without lifting efficacy, which stacks the model toward defensive avoidance instead of protective action."*

**Why this is the most valuable single import in the cluster.** It makes the ethical line and the effectiveness line **the same line.** Amplifying pain without raising the buyer's belief that something can be done does not merely cross an ethical boundary — it is predicted to fail, because the audience defends against the message rather than the problem. That collapses the usual ethics-versus-conversion trade-off, which is the strongest possible footing for a rule the skill must actually enforce.

**Operationalised as:** *never raise threat without raising efficacy in the same breath* — and the derived observation, which is `[S]`, that **this fails on layout as easily as on intent**: relief below the fold is fear control regardless of the copywriter's good faith.

**Also imported:** the split between **response efficacy** ("the fix works") and **self-efficacy** ("*you* can do this"). Practitioners routinely supply the first and neglect the second; EPPM treats them as equally load-bearing.

**Limitation.** Developed for health communication (smoking, vaccination, safety). Its transfer to commercial marketing is an extension, not a demonstrated result. Treat the *mechanism* as sound and the *magnitudes* as untested in this domain.

### 12.2 The persuasion / manipulation boundary
- **Author / org:** TARES framework (Baker & Martinson, as summarised); dark-patterns literature; practitioner sources
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296325002991 · https://www.taonique.com/blog/power-strategy/ethical-persuasion-in-marketing/ · https://www.merchantnorth.com/blog/the-ethics-of-dark-patterns-when-persuasive-design-crosses-a-line · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.00340
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Academic framework + practitioner + regulatory analysis · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4 / 3

**The autonomy criterion.** Persuasion *"supports autonomy by presenting clear and balanced information, enabling informed decision-making"*; manipulation *"undermines autonomy by subverting consent through deception or the exploitation of cognitive or emotional vulnerabilities."*

**The operational version, adopted verbatim into the skill because it is testable:** persuasion *"helps a customer see real value more clearly"*, while manipulation *"pushes a customer toward a decision they might not choose if they had **full information, emotional space, and a clear exit**."*

**Limitation.** The practitioner sources are content marketing for agencies selling ethical-marketing services. The academic and regulatory sources are stronger. Nothing here was read in full.

### 12.3 Laddering and Means-End Chain theory
- **Author / org:** Means-End Chain theory (Gutman); practitioner applications
- **URL:** https://www.driveresearch.com/market-research-company-blog/what-is-laddering-market-research/ · https://www.newtonx.com/article/unlocking-value-with-the-laddering-method/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Method, via practitioner summaries · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4 (theory) / 3 (summaries)

**The method.** Move the respondent from **features → benefits → motivational and emotional criteria**, by repeatedly asking why. Described by practitioners as *"the annoying back-seat child technique"* and as *"peeling back the proverbial onion."*

**Also imported from the adjacent complaint-analysis literature:** *"what might appear as the problem on the surface may actually be only a symptom of a much larger, underlying issue"* — and that fixing symptoms produces short-lived solutions where *"similar or new symptoms reappear."*

**Two `[S]` additions the sources do not make:**
1. **The stopping rules.** Too shallow = still about the product; too deep = a life theme. **The right depth is one rung above the life theme** — which is where the abstraction test (§9.1) also lands. Two instruments, one altitude.
2. **Laddering produces a tree, not a chain.** The 5 Whys is a root-cause tool for *defects* and assumes a single mechanical cause. Human motivation branches. **If ten people ladder from one complaint to three roots, that is three segments** — not noise to average away.

Every pain carries a value on each.

### 13.1 The Voice of the Customer — frequency is not importance
- **Author / org:** Abbie Griffin & John R. Hauser, *Marketing Science* 12(1), 1993, pp. 1–27
- **URL:** https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.12.1.1 · https://stuff.mit.edu/~hauser/Papers/NoteontheVoiceoftheCustomer.pdf
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Peer-reviewed academic · **Depth:** SNIPPET (abstract + summaries; full paper not read) · **Reliability:** 5

**The finding this skill needed.** The authors tested the hypothesis everyone quietly assumes — that customers mention their most important needs most often, so counting mentions could substitute for measuring importance.

> **"The data do not support this hypothesis — high-priority needs do not seem to be mentioned more often than low-priority needs."**

They concluded that formal research is required to establish priority; mention-frequency cannot stand in for it.

**Why this matters more here than almost anywhere else in the corpus.** Every "top pain points" list assembled by tallying reviews, comments or search volume is measuring **prevalence** and reporting it as **importance**. That is the default behaviour of an LLM asked to find pains, and it is wrong in a way that is invisible in the output.

**Adopted as:** prevalence and severity are measured separately, reported separately, and never summed — prevalence sits in the Commercial score, severity in the Strength score.

**Also imported:** roughly 20–30 customer interviews identify about 90% of the needs in a category, and **two analysts should code the transcripts because one analyst identifies only about half.** The second point is routinely ignored and is the cheaper of the two fixes.

**Limitation:** read at abstract and summary level. The 1993 study concerned portable food-carrying devices; generalisation across categories is the authors' claim, not an independently verified one here.

### 13.2 Opportunity Solution Trees and story-based interviews
- **Author / org:** Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*; Product Talk
- **URL:** https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/ · https://www.producttalk.org/sourcing-opportunities/
- **Language:** EN · **Type:** Practitioner framework · **Depth:** SNIPPET · **Reliability:** 4

**Definition adopted:** opportunities are *"the customer needs, pain points, and desires that, if addressed, will drive your desired outcome."* Note that this binds a pain to an **outcome** — a pain that drives no outcome you care about is not an opportunity, however real.

**Story-based interviewing:** *"prompting our customers to share a specific example of a past behavior rather than a generalized explanation"* — explicitly framed as bias mitigation. **Converges with the Mom Test from a different tradition**, which is why the skill states the rule once and enforces it everywhere.

### 13.4 The two-score correction — the brief's most valuable contribution
`[S — originating in the commissioning brief, not in a published source]`

The brief argued that a single pain score conflates two questions, and it was **correct about an error the skill had already made.** The previous PPS mixed severity, urgency, frequency and emotional intensity (how much it hurts) with willingness to pay, fit, solvability and messaging clarity (whether it is a market).

**Why this was a real defect and not a preference:** a 9/10 pain with 1/10 WTP and a 5/10 pain over ₽100M of exposure landed on comparable totals, with the distinction averaged away. **That is the averaging error the rest of this skill spends its time preventing** — committed by the skill's own instrument, on the skill's own subject matter.

Also originating in the brief and adopted: **pain concentration** (where does this become 5–10× worse), **pain threshold** (where the old solution stops scaling), **pain evolution** (maturity stages), the **behavioural evidence ladder** (complain → search → workaround → time → switch → pay → hire → budget → market), **cost of inaction** as a mandatory quantified field, **evidence AGAINST** as a mandatory column, and the **Pain × Segment matrix**.

**None of these carries a published source.** They are recorded as synthesis, and they are among the more useful things in the module — which is worth noting, because the corpus's strongest material is not uniformly its best-sourced material.
