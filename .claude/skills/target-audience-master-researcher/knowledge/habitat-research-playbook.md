# Habitat Research Playbook — How to Actually Find Where They Are

**Purpose.** Execution-level recipes for building a habitat map. The taxonomy of habitat *types* is in `../research/target-audience-research-report.md` §5. This file is the how.

**The standard:** a habitat is only named when you can state the **specific instance**. "LinkedIn" is a platform. "These six creators, this Slack group, the alumni list of this certification" is a habitat. Platform names in an audience report mean the research stopped early.

**The finishing test:** *"If I had to reach 100 of these people by Friday, exactly where would I go and what would I do?"* Until that has a concrete answer, the map is not done.

---

## Phase 1 — Ask (highest yield, lowest effort)

This outperforms every tool and every search operator. Put these in **every** interview:

1. "What do you read every week?"
2. "Which chats or groups are you in?"
3. "Whose posts do you never skip?"
4. "Where did you look the last time you had this problem?"
5. "Where do people in your position talk shop?"
6. "What conference or event do you actually go to?"
7. "Who did you ask for a recommendation last time?"

**Record answers verbatim and count them.** Three interviewees naming the same venue is stronger evidence of density than any subscriber number.

---

## Phase 2 — Trace

Every named venue has four things attached to it. Follow all four:

| Attached to a venue | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Members / subscribers** | The segment, enumerable |
| **Sponsors / advertisers** | Who else has already validated this audience commercially |
| **Comment sections** | The real opinions, and who the recognised voices are |
| **Neighbours** | Venues the same people also belong to |

**The sponsor read is the most under-used.** A newsletter's or podcast's sponsor list is a free map of who is already paying to reach this exact audience — and therefore of whether the audience is commercially proven.

### The three that punch above their weight

**Job boards, freelance marketplaces and tender platforms are the most under-used sources in this entire table**, and the only free ones that routinely reach `L5–L6`.

The logic is direct: if a company is paying someone **₽150,000/month to do X**, that single posting simultaneously proves the problem exists, that it is worth ~₽1.8M/year, that budget is approved, and who to contact — all timestamped and public. Two hundred completed orders for the same task on a freelance marketplace is transaction evidence that no survey can match.

Most audience research stops at Reddit, which caps at `L3`. **These three are how you get to `L5` without spending anything.**

### ⚠ SparkToro geographic limits — read before relying on it

SparkToro's own documentation states it covers **English-language audiences in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom**, with expansion hoped for in 2027. Coverage is described as reasonably decent for US, UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia and South Africa; **outside those, data is less reliable because audience sizes are too small.** Demographics are withheld unless 50–100+ matching profiles exist. `[E]`

**Consequence for RU, UA and most of Europe: do not extrapolate SparkToro output to these markets.** For RU/CIS habitat work, the equivalent stack is TGStat + VK search + Wordstat + vc.ru/Habr, assembled manually. There is no single tool that substitutes.

## Phase 3 — Search recipes by surface

### 3.1 Reddit

```
site:reddit.com "[problem phrase]"
site:reddit.com "[competitor name]" alternative
site:reddit.com "[role]" "how do you"
site:reddit.com/r/[suspected sub] "[problem]"
```

**Workflow:**
1. Build a keyword list: the problem, its synonyms, abbreviations, and competitor names.
2. Run each through Reddit search **and** plain Google `site:` — Google's index is better than Reddit's own search.
3. Sort by top of all time, then by new, then by most comments. The three lists differ meaningfully.
4. For each promising subreddit record: **subscribers · posts per day · comment-to-post ratio · rules on self-promotion**.
5. Read **comments, not posts.** Posts are performances; comments are opinions.
6. Isolate the four conversation types: **Pain Points · Solution Requests · Money Talk · Hot Discussions.**
7. Build a **repeat-question log with dates** — the same question recurring monthly is frequency evidence.

**Tool note.** GummySearch was the standard tool here and is reported to be shutting down. Verify before recommending it or any successor; the manual workflow above does not depend on it.

---

### 3.2 Telegram (primary RU/CIS surface)

**Discovery:**
1. **TGStat** — the working catalogue, with per-channel statistics, sorted by country and topic.
2. Telegram's own search for exact problem phrases and role names.
3. Ask interviewees directly — closed chats are invisible from outside and are often the best ones.
4. Look at which channels the channels you found **cross-promote with** (взаимопиар reveals the neighbourhood).

**Assessment — per channel or chat:**
```
Name · Subscribers · Posts/week · Views per post · COMMENTS PER POST · Rules on promotion · Who answers questions · How to get in
```

**The critical metric is comment activity, not subscriber count.** A 40,000-subscriber channel with dead comments is a broadcast. A 900-member chat with 60 messages a day is a community, and it is worth far more.

**Watch for:** paid placements in channels with inflated metrics — a well-known grey market. Cross-check view counts against subscriber counts and against TGStat's own data.

---

### 3.3 LinkedIn (primary B2B surface)

**Discovery:**
- Search by role + company size + geography using the native filters.
- Search **job changes** in the target role — a trigger event, and a public one.
- Read the **comment sections** on category posts, not the posts.

**The check most people skip:** are the people engaging with category content **buyers** or **peers**? Look at the commenters' job titles. Content that performs among competitors and never reaches buyers is the peer-applause trap, and it feels exactly like success.

**Record:** the 5–10 creators whose *commenters* are your buyers. Those are the habitat. Follower count is not.

---

### 3.4 Communities: Discord, Slack, forums

```
"[niche]" discord server
"[role]" slack community
site:[forum domain] "[problem phrase]"
"[industry]" forum "[problem]"
```

**Assessment:**
- **Member count vs. daily active** — the ratio matters more than either number.
- **Vetting on entry** — a community that vets is worth more, and a referral requirement is itself a density signal.
- **Rules on vendors** — read them before doing anything. One bad pitch ends access permanently.
- **Who answers questions** — those people are the segment's trusted nodes.

**Forums specifically:** search the **archive**. A ten-year-old thread still receiving replies is a permanent unmet need, and forums are unfashionable enough that competition there is unusually low.

---

### 3.5 Search engines

**EN:** Ahrefs, Semrush, or free alternatives.
**RU:** Яндекс Wordstat (volume, region, seasonality) + Google Trends (direction). Free, and the evidence a RU client will accept.

**Classify every query into three buckets** — the ratio between them is the market's awareness distribution:
| Bucket | Example shape | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-phrased | "why does X keep happening" | Problem-aware |
| Solution-phrased | "best tool for X" | Solution-aware |
| Brand-phrased | "[competitor] pricing" | Product-aware or better |

**The intent tell:** does the SERP carry ads and shopping features? If yes, advertisers have already validated that the query converts.

**Also harvest:** autocomplete suggestions · "people also ask" · related searches. These are free question inventories for content.

---

### 3.6 Review sites and marketplaces

**Discovery:**
```
site:g2.com "[category]"
site:capterra.com "[category]"
"[competitor]" reviews
"[competitor]" vs
"alternatives to [competitor]"
```

**RU:** Otzovik · marketplace reviews (Wildberries, Ozon, Yandex Market) · Yandex Maps and 2GIS for local.

**What you are looking for** *(beyond pain themes)*: reviewers who are **clearly not the intended customer**. "Great for agencies, useless for in-house teams like mine" is an unaddressed segment describing itself in public.

---

### 3.7 Ad libraries

**Meta Ad Library** — free, public, searchable.

**Workflow:**
1. Search each competitor.
2. **Sort by start date.** Ads running 60–90+ days are almost certainly profitable — nobody keeps paying for losers.
3. For each long-runner, extract: the **angle**, the **promise**, the **implied audience**.
4. Note which segments they have **stopped** advertising to — an abandoned segment is a fact about their economics, worth understanding before you enter it.

**Other libraries** *(assumed by analogy — not verified in the corpus, so verify before relying on them):* Google Ads Transparency Center · TikTok Creative Center · ad-spy services for VK and Yandex.

---

### 3.8 Job boards (trigger discovery)

**EN:** LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed. **RU:** hh.ru.

**Search by duties, not by titles.** Titles vary between companies; duties do not.

```
[duty keyword] + [industry] + [region]
"[the manual task your product replaces]"
```

**What each posting gives you, for free:**
| Element | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| The duties | The problem, in their words |
| The salary | **The budget** |
| The date | The freshness of the trigger |
| Repeated reposting | They cannot fill it — an even stronger signal |
| The contact | A named person |

**Handle with care.** Never frame outreach as "don't hire, buy us" — it reads as insulting. Frame around helping the new hire succeed, or around the gap until they start.

---

### 3.9 Institutional surfaces

- **Associations and licensing bodies** — member directories are often public. These are the *formalized attributes* made free: role, licence, certification, industry code.
- **Conferences** — you do not need to attend to learn from the agenda. **The speaker list is a free map of the segment's influencers; the sponsor list is a free map of who else is selling to them, and at what level of commitment.**
- **Public registers** — corporate, licensing, certification.
- **Tender platforms** — read **past awards** to learn who buys, at what price, and from whom. A free pricing and competitive archive.

---

### 3.10 Freelance platforms as a demand feed

Upwork, Kwork, FL.ru and similar are one of the cleanest free demand datasets in any market, whether or not you ever bid.

**Read job posts for:**
- **Repeated job types** → an unserved, productisable need
- **Budget ranges** → willingness to pay, by segment
- **Client history** → who buys repeatedly
- **The wording of the request** → the exact vocabulary of a buyer in motion

## Phase 4 — Assess density

For every candidate habitat, record all eight fields. A habitat missing the last two is not usable.

```
NAME:              ____________________
TYPE:              community / search / media / institutional / borrowed
SIZE:              ____________________
ACTIVITY:          posts per day ___ · comments per post ___
AUDIENCE MATCH:    what % are actually our segment? ___
VENDOR RULES:      ____________________
KEY VOICES:        ____________________
ROUTE IN:          ____________________
FREE / PAID:       ____________________
FIRST ACTION:      ____________________
```

### Over-index beats raw share

**The most common habitat-research error is ranking venues by how many of the audience are present.** That ranking is almost always useless, because it just rediscovers the biggest platforms.

```
OVER-INDEX  =  segment's usage rate  ÷  baseline population usage rate
```

A worked illustration from a published audience brief on mid-market RevOps leaders: `[E]`

| Venue | Share of segment | vs. baseline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | **80%+ monthly** | ~parity | **Table stakes.** Tells you nothing. Everyone in B2B is there, including every competitor |
| Slack | **33.2% monthly** | **+201% over-index** | **The finding.** They are distinctively there |
| Substack newsletters | notable share | over-indexed | Guest essays, sponsorships, creator partnerships |

The raw numbers say LinkedIn. The over-index says Slack. **The over-index is the actionable one**, because it identifies where this audience is *unusual*, which is where you can reach them without competing against the entire market for attention.

**Bands:**

| Over-index | Reading |
|---|---|
| < 120% | Table stakes. Be present, expect no advantage |
| 120–150% | Mild affinity. Worth testing |
| **> 150%** | **Distinctive. Prioritise** |
| > 300% | Defining. This venue is close to a proxy for the segment itself |

**Where to get the baseline.** Ad platform audience estimators, published platform statistics, or your own cross-segment comparison. When no baseline is available, compare the segment against a *different* segment you have researched — relative over-index is still informative.

### The unreachable-but-influential habitat

Some high-over-index venues cannot be advertised in at all — private Slack workspaces, closed Telegram chats, internal channels. **This does not make them useless; it changes the mechanic.**

If a segment lives in workspaces you cannot enter, the play is to **create content designed to be forwarded into them.** A link that someone pastes into their team's channel reaches the venue with a colleague's implicit endorsement attached — which is stronger than any ad placement you could have bought there.

Design for it explicitly: a self-contained artefact, an obvious reason to share, a title that makes the sharer look informed, and no gate in front of it.

### The density verdict

| Finding | Verdict | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 3 venues cover most of the segment | **Dense** | Free acquisition is arithmetically viable |
| 10+ venues, little overlap | **Fragmented** | Content reaches noise; paid targeting or change segment |
| No venue found after a real search | **Scattered** | Reachability gate fails — say so |

## Phase 5 — The output

A habitat map is a table, not a paragraph. Ranked by *audience match × accessibility*, never by size.

| # | Habitat | Type | Size | Match | Rules | Route in | Free? | First action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | | | |

**Then answer the Friday test explicitly:**

> *"To reach 100 of them by Friday we would: ______________."*

If it cannot be answered, write that plainly. **"This segment has no findable habitat" is a research finding and a reachability verdict** — not a failure to try harder.

## Ethics and compliance — non-negotiable

- **Read community rules before acting.** They vary per venue and are enforced.
- **Contribute before promoting.** Communities detect extraction, and the punishment is permanent.
- **Do not scrape** where it violates terms of service or local law.
- **Comply with anti-spam law** in the relevant jurisdiction for any outreach.
- **Do not join closed communities under false pretences.** Beyond the ethics, it is the single fastest way to destroy the credibility you came to build.
- **Public data used for outreach is still personal data.** Reference what someone published; do not compile dossiers.

The habitat is a place where real people are talking to each other. Behaving as though it is a lead list is both wrong and, in practice, self-defeating.
