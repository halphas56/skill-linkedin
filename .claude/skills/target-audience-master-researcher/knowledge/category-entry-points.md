# Category Entry Points

**A segment generator that works through situations instead of people.** Most audience research asks *"who buys this?"* CEP analysis asks *"in what situation does someone think of this category at all?"* — and the second question routinely produces segments the first one cannot see.

> **"Category entry points are not about the brand, they're about the buyer."**
> — Jenni Romaniuk, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute `[E]`

---

## §1 — What a CEP is

A Category Entry Point is any **thought, need, or moment that leads a person to consider a purchase in a category.** `[E — Ehrenberg-Bass]`

Examples: *"something quick to drink after the gym"* · *"software to invoice without an accountant"*.

The underlying claim from Ehrenberg-Bass: **"the most important search engine is still the one in your mind."** `[E — LinkedIn B2B Institute]` Most purchases start with memory, not with a search box. The situation arrives first; retrieval from memory happens next; only then does anyone open a browser. Whoever is remembered at that moment has already won most of the contest.

For this skill, the operational consequence is different from the branding one: **each CEP is a candidate segment.**

---

## §2 — CEP vs. JTBD — they are not the same instrument

They overlap enough to be confused and differ enough that using the wrong one wastes a week.

| | **JTBD** (`jtbd-engine.md`) | **CEP** (this file) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | What progress are they trying to make? | In what situation does the category come to mind? |
| Origin | Christensen / Moesta — demand-side innovation | Ehrenberg-Bass — mental availability |
| Depth vs. breadth | **Deep** on one struggling moment | **Broad** across all buying situations |
| Method | ~10 switch interviews, backwards timeline | Survey or structured brainstorm across the 7Ws |
| Output | One rich, buildable segment definition | A map of 10–30 situations, each a candidate |
| Best for | A challenger choosing a beachhead | Mapping the full opportunity space; finding uncontested situations |
| Failure if used alone | Miss the breadth of the market | Shallow — knows *when*, not *why* |

**Use both, in this order:**

1. **CEP first, to generate.** Map the situations. Each is a segment candidate. Cheap, fast, and it surfaces contexts nobody in the category is addressing.
2. **JTBD second, to define.** Take the two or three most promising CEPs and go deep — six-link chain, four forces, five rings.

CEP is the wide-angle lens; JTBD is the macro. Running JTBD without CEP means going deep on the first situation you happened to think of.

## §3 — Generating CEPs: the 7W framework

Romaniuk's framework from *Better Brand Health*. `[E]` Work through every W; each one produces situations the others miss.

| W | Question | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Why** | What motivation or goal brings this on? | Functional and emotional entry points |
| **When** | Time of day, week, month, year; what precedes it? | Timing and seasonality; ad scheduling |
| **Where** | Physical or digital location | Channel and context; local segmentation |
| **While** | What else are they doing at the time? | Format constraints; co-occurring needs |
| **With / For Whom** | Alone, with others, or **on behalf of someone else**? | **Buyer ≠ user detection** — see §5 |
| **With What** | What else is involved — other products, tools, systems? | Partnerships, integrations, bundles |
| **hoW-feeling** | What emotional state are they in? | Tone, register, and urgency |

The LinkedIn B2B Institute uses a compressed version — **who, what, when, where, why** — and notes it can be run either as a survey or as a structured brainstorm with sales and marketing in the room. `[E]` For a fast pass, the compressed set is enough; for a real map, run all seven.

**Method.** For each W, generate freely, then merge duplicates. A healthy category map lands somewhere around 15–30 distinct CEPs. Fewer than 10 means you stopped early.

## §4 — Worked example: a massage gun

The consumer question *"who buys a massage gun?"* produces "men 25–45 interested in fitness" — a demographic non-answer.

The CEP question *"in what situation does someone think about a massage gun?"* produces this:

| # | CEP (situation) | Why | With/For whom | Feeling | Implied segment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | After a workout | Recovery | Self | Accomplished, sore | Regular gym-goers, amateur athletes |
| 2 | After a long day at a desk | Relief from stiffness | Self | Tired, achy | Office workers, remote workers |
| 3 | Pain has appeared | Fix something wrong | Self | Worried | People with a specific complaint — highest urgency |
| 4 | **Need a gift for parents** | Show care | **Someone else** | Obligation + affection | **Buyer ≠ user.** Entirely different segment |
| 5 | Want to relax | Comfort ritual | Self / partner | Stressed | Wellness-oriented consumers |
| 6 | **A doctor recommended massage** | Follow medical advice | Self | Compliant, motivated | Referred patients — highest trust, lowest price sensitivity |

Six situations. **Each has a different message, a different channel, a different urgency, and in two cases a different person entirely.**

Read what falls out:

- **CEP 3 and CEP 6** carry the highest urgency and the lowest price sensitivity — pain and medical advice both suppress haggling.
- **CEP 4** is a *different buyer from the user*. Gifting has its own season, its own channels, its own objections ("will they actually use it?"), and its own packaging requirements. A gift-market strategy and a fitness-market strategy share only the product.
- **CEP 6** implies a partnership channel — physiotherapists and clinics — that no amount of demographic analysis would surface.
- **CEP 1 and 2** are the crowded ones. Every competitor is there.

**This is the mechanism:** situations generate segments that people-first thinking cannot reach.

## §5 — Buyer ≠ user detection

The **With / For Whom** dimension is the cheapest reliable detector of the buyer/user split — one of the most consequential and most-missed distinctions in audience work.

Whenever a CEP has "for someone else" in it, you have found a segment where:

| | User | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Cares about | Whether it works | Whether it *looks* like it works, and how it reflects on them |
| Objection | "Is this the right one?" | "Will they actually use it? Will they think I chose well?" |
| Proof needed | Function, reviews | Presentation, returnability, social safety |
| Channel | Category search | Gift guides, seasonal, recommendation |
| Season | Year-round | Concentrated on dates |

Common "for someone else" CEPs: gifts · buying for a child, a parent, a partner · **a manager buying tooling for a team who will use it** · procurement buying on behalf of a department · a doctor, consultant or agency specifying on a client's behalf.

**The B2B version is the buying group.** A complex B2B purchase involves 6–10 people `[E]` — every one of whom is "buying for someone else" in some sense. Map them separately or you will write for a seat that does not decide.

## §6 — Prioritising CEPs: the 3C framework

Once mapped, narrow. The LinkedIn B2B Institute's screen: `[E]`

| C | Question | Scored |
|---|---|---|
| **Commonness** | How often does this situation actually occur? | Frequency × market size |
| **Competition** | How crowded is this situation already? | Who owns it in memory today? |
| **Credibility** | How believable is *our* connection to it? | Can we own it honestly? |

### The challenger play

For an established leader, the CEP strategy is breadth: attach to as many situations as possible, because **brands with broader and fresher CEP networks show higher customer acquisition and retention.** `[E]`

**For everyone else — a startup, a service business, a challenger — the play is the opposite and it is the reason this file exists:**

> **Find a CEP with high Commonness, low Competition and high Credibility for you, and own it completely.**

You will not out-remember the incumbent on the biggest CEP. You can be the only brand attached to a smaller one. In the massage-gun example, every competitor owns "after a workout"; almost nobody owns "my doctor told me to." The second is smaller, uncontested, higher-trust, and far cheaper to win.

**The scoring shortcut:**

```
CEP PRIORITY  =  Commonness × Credibility ÷ Competition
```

High-commonness, high-competition CEPs are where budget goes to die for anyone without incumbent-scale distribution.

## §7 — B2B category entry points

B2B CEPs are usually **events inside an organisation**, which makes them observable from outside — and therefore targetable. This is where CEP analysis and trigger-event research converge.

| B2B CEP | Observable signal | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor contract is up for renewal | Renewal cycles, public contract terms | Tender archives, LinkedIn posts |
| A new executive arrived | Role changes | LinkedIn, press releases |
| We failed an audit / got a finding | Rarely public — surfaces in interviews | Interviews, forums |
| We can't fill a role | **A repeatedly reposted vacancy** | Job boards |
| Entering a new market or geography | Announcements, new entity registration | Registries, press |
| The board asked for a number we can't produce | Surfaces in interviews and forums | Communities |
| A key person left | Vacancy for a replacement | Job boards |
| Rapid growth broke the process | Funding, headcount spikes | Funding databases, LinkedIn |
| Regulation changed | Public, dated, industry-wide | Regulatory calendars |
| A client demanded something we can't do | Procurement questionnaires; surfaces in sales calls | Sales calls |

**The B2B advantage:** unlike consumer CEPs, most of these are **public and timestamped**. That converts a CEP map into an outbound queue — see `habitat-research-playbook.md` and the trigger-event method.

## §8 — From CEP to segment

A CEP is a candidate, not a segment. Convert it:

1. **State the situation** in the buyer's words, not the category's.
2. **Identify who is in it** — and check whether the buyer is the user (§5).
3. **Find the observable signal** that someone has entered it. No signal → not targetable → the CEP is a message angle, not a segment.
4. **Run the JTBD chain** on it (`jtbd-engine.md` §2) to get depth.
5. **Score it** in the matrix like any other candidate. **A CEP-derived segment gets no discount for being clever.**
6. **Check Competition** — who already owns this situation in memory?

**The disqualifier:** a CEP with no observable entry signal cannot be targeted, only broadcast to. For a business with a budget that may be fine. For one without, it is a dead end, and it should be reported as a positioning angle rather than an acquisition segment.

## §9 — Honest limits

**The Ehrenberg-Bass tension.** CEP theory comes from the same school that argues *against* narrow targeting — brands grow by reaching more category buyers, especially light ones. `[E — Byron Sharp]` This skill uses CEPs for the opposite purpose: to find a narrow, ownable situation. That is a legitimate borrowing of the mechanism, but be aware you are using a breadth instrument for a focus decision, and say so if challenged.

**Regime matters** (`../research/target-audience-research-report.md` §0):

| Regime | CEP use |
|---|---|
| Established brand, mass category, distribution solved | **Breadth** — attach to as many CEPs as possible. The orthodox use |
| Challenger, startup, service business, no distribution | **Focus** — own one uncontested CEP completely |
| B2B with observable triggers | **Queue** — convert CEPs into a monitored outbound list |

**Other limits:**
- CEP mapping done as a brainstorm is `L1` (`evidence-ladder.md`). It becomes `L3+` only when confirmed by survey, search data, or interviews. A whiteboard full of situations is a hypothesis list.
- Rigorous CEP measurement in the Ehrenberg-Bass tradition requires surveys most small businesses will never run. Use the logic qualitatively and label the level honestly.
- CEPs decay. Situations change with technology, regulation and habit. Re-map yearly.

## §10 — Quality gate

- [ ] All **7 Ws** worked through, not just why/when
- [ ] **10+ distinct CEPs** generated before narrowing
- [ ] Every CEP stated as a **situation**, never as a person type
- [ ] **With/For Whom** checked on every CEP → buyer ≠ user flagged
- [ ] **3C scored** — Commonness, Competition, Credibility
- [ ] For each shortlisted CEP: the **observable entry signal** is named, or the CEP is reclassified as a message angle
- [ ] Top CEPs taken through the **JTBD chain** for depth
- [ ] **Regime named** — breadth play or focus play, and why
- [ ] Every CEP carries an **evidence level**; brainstormed CEPs marked `L1`
- [ ] At least one CEP identified that **no competitor currently owns**

### The test

Point at your CEP map and ask: **"which of these situations does no competitor own, and could we own it honestly?"**

If every situation on the map is already owned, you are not looking at enough situations. Go back to the 7Ws — particularly *While*, *With What* and *hoW-feeling*, which are the three most often skipped and the three most likely to be uncontested.
