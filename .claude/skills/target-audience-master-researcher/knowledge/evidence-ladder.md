# The Evidence Ladder

**The core protocol of this skill.** Every claim about a client's audience carries a level from **L0 to L6**. No exceptions, no unlabelled assertions.

**Why this exists.** An LLM doing audience research fails in one specific, predictable way: it invents beautiful, plausible, entirely fictional pains, writes them fluently, and the fluency gets mistaken for evidence. The ladder makes that failure visible instead of invisible. It is the single highest-leverage rule in the skill.

---

## §1 — Two marking systems, do not confuse them

They are orthogonal axes and both are required.

| System | Applies to | Question it answers | Where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`[E]` / `[S]` / `[A]`** | *Methodological* claims — statements about how marketing works | "Is this framework sourced, synthesised, or assumed?" | `../research/` — the corpus |
| **`L0`–`L6`** | *Audience* claims — statements about this client's actual buyers | "How strongly is this particular claim about these particular people supported?" | Every engagement deliverable |

"Ulwick's opportunity algorithm weights importance twice" is `[E]`. "Dental clinic owners find compliance reporting painful" is `L2` or `L4` depending entirely on what you found. Never label an audience claim `[E]` — it hides the ladder.

---

## §2 — The underlying principle

**Evidence strength equals what it cost the person to produce it.**

This single idea generates the whole ladder, and it lets you place any new signal you encounter without consulting the table.

| Signal | Cost to produce | Therefore |
|---|---|---|
| An opinion in a survey | ~0 seconds, no stake | Nearly worthless alone |
| A comment | seconds, tiny social stake | Weak but real |
| A search query | intent + a moment of need | Meaningful |
| A forum question written out in detail | minutes + admitting ignorance publicly | Stronger |
| A workaround they built themselves | hours of unpaid labour | Strong |
| A job posting at $3,000/month | an approved budget line + headcount | Very strong |
| An actual purchase | money, irreversibly | Strongest |

When you meet a signal type not in this document, ask **"what did this cost them?"** and place it accordingly.

## §3 — The seven levels

### L0 — Speculation
**Definition.** Generated by the model, or asserted by the founder, with no external artefact behind it.

**Detection.** Ask: *"Where exactly did I see this?"* If the answer is "it follows logically" or "the client said so," it is L0.

**Licenses.** Nothing. Not a single decision.

**Mandatory handling.** Label `L0` explicitly, or delete it. **The cardinal sin of this skill is laundering L0 into L2 by rewriting it more confidently.** A well-phrased invention is still an invention — fluency is not evidence.

> A founder's belief about their own market is L0 until corroborated. This is not disrespect; it is the reason they hired research. Their confidence correlates with proximity to the problem, not with the size of the market.

---

### L1 — Plausible
**Definition.** Consistent with known structural facts, mechanically sensible, but still unobserved.

**Example.** "Clinics that just received a licence renewal notice probably feel time pressure." Nobody said it; the mechanism is sound.

**Licenses.** A hypothesis worth the cost of testing. Nothing more.

**Handling.** Label `L1` and attach the cheapest experiment that would move it to L2.

---

### L2 — Observed
**Definition.** At least one real artefact exists: a comment, a review, a forum post, a support ticket, a sales-call transcript, a search query.

**Example.** "One G2 reviewer wrote that onboarding took three weeks and nearly killed the rollout."

**Licenses.** A research direction. **Not** a strategy, a channel, or a budget.

**Handling.** Cite the artefact verbatim with its source and date. One observation is one observation — the classic error is treating a vivid single quote as a pattern because it was memorable.

---

### L3 — Repeated
**Definition.** The same pattern appears in **3+ independent instances**, from the same source *type*.

**Example.** "Eleven of thirty G2 reviews across four competitors complain about onboarding length."

**Licenses.** A working assumption. Safe to build a message hypothesis on; not yet safe to bet a budget.

**Caution.** Repetition within one source type can be an artefact of that source. Eleven Reddit threads may reflect one community's culture rather than a market. That is precisely what L4 exists to fix.

---

### L4 — Triangulated
**Definition.** Confirmed across **3+ independent source types**. This is the level at which a claim becomes strategy-grade.

**Example.** Onboarding pain appears in (a) competitor reviews, (b) search volume on "how to migrate from X", and (c) two of five customer interviews. Three different mechanisms, three different populations, same finding.

**Licenses.** A strategy decision. Segment selection, positioning, channel choice.

> **"The best research comes from triangulation, or using multiple methods to verify findings."** [E — SparkToro, `../research/source-map.md` §9.5]

**The independence test is where this level is won or lost.** See §4.

---

### L5 — Behavioural
**Definition.** People are spending **time or effort**, not just words. Their behaviour is visible and costly.

**Examples.**
- Sustained search volume with commercial-intent modifiers
- Workarounds they built themselves (spreadsheets, scripts, Zapier chains)
- Job postings for a role that does the work your product does
- Applications, waitlist signups, event attendance
- A competitor's ad running 90+ days (someone's money says it converts)

**Licenses.** Channel investment. Building. Hiring against it.

**Why it outranks L4.** Triangulation proves *many people say it*. Behaviour proves *people act on it*. Saying is free; acting is not.

---

### L6 — Transactional
**Definition.** Money has already changed hands for this problem, by these people.

**Examples.**
- Your own closed-won deals in the segment
- Competitors with visible paying customers at a known price
- Filled orders on a freelance marketplace for exactly this task
- Awarded tenders with contract values
- A course selling out at a stated price

**Licenses.** Full commitment.

**Note.** L6 answers *ability and willingness to pay* directly — the two criteria most often assumed and least often verified. See `willingness-to-pay.md`.

#### L6 has two floors: the Big Hire and the Little Hire

A purchase is not the end of the evidence chain. Christensen splits it: `[E]`

| | **Big Hire** | **Little Hire** |
|---|---|---|
| What | The purchase | Actually using it |
| Tracked by | Almost all sales data | Almost nothing |
| Proves | They were willing to buy | **The job was real** |

> *"The Big Hire might suggest that a product has solved a customer's job, but only a consistent series of Little Hires can confirm it."*

The dress bought with the tag still on. The app downloaded and opened once. A vendor counting downloads has no idea whether the job was solved.

**Operationally:** `L6-big` (a transaction occurred) is strong. **`L6-little` (repeated use over time) is the strongest evidence this ladder can reach**, because it survives the moment of persuasion. When assessing an existing base, ask for *usage* data, not *purchase* data — and treat a high Big Hire rate with a low Little Hire rate as a warning that the job was mis-identified and the buyers will churn.

## §4 — Independence: the rule that makes triangulation real

**Triangulation is worthless if the "independent" sources are not independent.** This is where most research quietly fails.

Two sources are independent only when they differ on **at least two** of these three axes:

| Axis | Independent | NOT independent |
|---|---|---|
| **Source type** | A review vs. a job posting vs. an interview | Three review sites |
| **Population** | Reddit users vs. conference attendees vs. your CRM | Three threads in one subreddit |
| **Mechanism** | Stated (interview) vs. behavioural (search) vs. transactional (purchase) | Three surveys |

### Three failures of false independence

1. **The citation chain.** Five blog posts all quoting one original study is **one source**, not five. Trace every claim to its origin before counting it.
2. **The echo community.** Three threads in the same community share its culture, its vocabulary and its biases. That is one population.
3. **The single-mechanism stack.** A survey, an interview and a poll are all *stated preference*. They will agree with each other and be wrong together — because people are unreliable narrators of their own behaviour in a consistent direction.

> SparkToro states the underlying problem bluntly: **"People are terrible at accurately reporting their own behavior."** [E — `../research/source-map.md` §9.5]

**The strongest triangulation pairs one stated source with one behavioural source with one transactional source.** If all three of your sources are people talking, you have L3 wearing an L4 badge.

---

## §5 — The counting rule

From the brief, and now binding:

```
1 independent source   →  HYPOTHESIS       (L1–L2)
2 independent sources  →  WEAK EVIDENCE    (L3)
3+ independent sources →  VALIDATED PATTERN (L4)
```

Plus the cost overlay:

```
+ people are spending time/effort   →  L5
+ people are spending money         →  L6
```

## §6 — What each level licenses

**Match the decision's cost to the evidence's level.** Spending real money on L2 is the expensive failure; refusing to act at L5 is the slow one.

| Decision | Minimum level | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Add a segment to the candidate list | **L1** | Generation should be generous; filtering happens later |
| Write a message hypothesis | **L2** | You need their actual words |
| Prioritise a segment in the scoring matrix | **L3** | A total built on L1s is arithmetic on fiction |
| Recommend a segment as the primary target | **L4** | This is a strategy decision |
| Commit budget to a channel | **L4** + a costly-action test | Money needs corroboration and a live signal |
| Build or significantly change the product | **L5** | Behaviour, not opinion |
| Reprice, or bet the company | **L6** | Only transactions prove willingness to pay |
| Tell a client a segment is *bad* | **L3 minimum** | Disqualification needs evidence too — "I couldn't find anything" is a finding about your search, not about the market |

That last row matters. **An absence of evidence must be reported as an absence of evidence**, not converted into a negative conclusion. "No search volume found" and "no demand exists" are different statements, and for a genuinely new market they routinely diverge.

## §7 — How to write a levelled claim

**Format:** `claim [L#: source · n · date]`

**Good:**
> Onboarding length is the dominant switching trigger in this category `[L4: 11/30 G2 reviews across 4 vendors · 2 of 5 interviews · 320/mo search on "migrate from {competitor}" · Aug 2026]`

> Clinic owners feel acute pressure in the 30 days before licence renewal `[L1: mechanism plausible, no artefact found — test via 5 interviews]`

**Unacceptable:**
> Clinic owners are frustrated by complex compliance software and want something simpler.

That last one is L0 wearing the clothes of a finding. It has no source, no count, no date — and it is exactly what an LLM produces by default.

## §8 — The Evidence Profile (mandatory in every deliverable)

Every audience report carries this block. It takes four lines and it is the difference between a research asset and a confident-sounding document.

```
EVIDENCE PROFILE
Claims total:        ___
L0 speculation:      ___   (should be 0 in a delivered report)
L1 plausible:        ___
L2 observed:         ___
L3 repeated:         ___
L4 triangulated:     ___
L5 behavioural:      ___
L6 transactional:    ___

Highest level supporting the PRIMARY recommendation:  L__
Independent source types used: ___  (list them)
Weakest load-bearing assumption: ______________________
```

### Reading it

- **Any L0 in a delivered report is a defect.** Fix or delete before delivery.
- **Primary recommendation below L4** → the deliverable is a *research plan*, and must say so in its opening line. That is a legitimate and often correct output.
- **Fewer than 3 independent source types** → triangulation has not occurred, whatever the individual claims say.
- **A matrix mostly at L1** → you have produced a well-formatted hypothesis. Label it honestly; do not let the table's precision imply confidence the evidence cannot carry.

## §8b — The three types of fit, mapped

Osterwalder's fit stages are a useful external check on where a project actually stands. `[E]`

| Fit | Achieved when | Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem–solution** *(on paper)* | Facts confirm customers have these jobs, pains and gains, and you have designed against them. **No evidence yet that anyone wants your solution** | `L3–L4` |
| **Product–market** *(in the market)* | Facts confirm the offer creates value and is gaining traction | `L5–L6` |
| **Business model** *(in the bank)* | Facts confirm it can be delivered profitably and at scale | Economics proven |

Note the honesty built into the first row by the authors themselves: *"соответствие пока не подтверждено фактами и существует только на бумаге."* **A well-researched profile plus a designed proposition is still a hypothesis.**

### Test the circle before the square

A sequencing rule with a diagnostic reason: `[E]`

> **If you smoke-test an offer and it fails, you cannot tell whether the offer was wrong or the audience was wrong.**

So test the **circle** (the customer profile — which jobs, pains and gains genuinely matter) *before* the **square** (the offer). Most validation advice jumps straight to the square, which is why so many "we tested it and it didn't work" conclusions are uninterpretable.

**Requirement for this skill:** every validation plan includes at least one experiment testing **the audience profile itself**, run before any offer test. See `customer-profile.md` §10.

## §9 — Escalation: cheapest route up the ladder

When a load-bearing claim sits too low, this is the cheapest way to raise it.

| From → To | Cheapest move | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| L0 → L1 | State the mechanism explicitly and check it is not contradicted by known facts | minutes |
| L1 → L2 | Find one artefact: search the exact phrasing on Reddit, G2, a forum, Wordstat | 30 min |
| L2 → L3 | Count. 20–30 reviews, or a repeat-question log across a community's archive | 2–3 hours |
| L3 → L4 | **Add a different source *type*** — ideally a behavioural one. Search data, job postings, ad longevity | 2–4 hours |
| L4 → L5 | Look for spent effort: workarounds, job postings, applications, 90-day ads | 2–4 hours |
| L5 → L6 | Look for spent money: competitor customers, marketplace orders, awarded tenders, your own closed-won | 2–4 hours |
| Any → L6 directly | **Run a costly-action test.** A deposit, a paid pilot, a pre-order | 1–2 weeks |

**Note the asymmetry:** L3 → L4 is the highest-value single move in the entire process, and it is usually only a few hours of work. It is also the step most often skipped, because L3 already *feels* convincing.

## §10 — Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidence laundering** | Rewriting an L0 guess in the register of a finding | Label the level. Fluency is not evidence |
| **Quote inflation** | One vivid quote presented as a pattern | One quote is L2. Count before you conclude |
| **False triangulation** | Three sources that share a type, a population, or a mechanism | Apply the §4 independence test |
| **Citation chains** | Five secondary sources, one origin | Trace to origin; count once |
| **Stated-preference stacking** | Survey + interview + poll, all agreeing | Add a behavioural source or stay at L3 |
| **Absence as negative** | "No data found" → "no demand exists" | Report absence as absence. Note the market type |
| **Level drift** | A claim enters at L2 in section 4 and is cited as fact in section 11 | Carry the level with the claim everywhere it appears |
| **Client-base bias** | Treating existing customers as the ideal audience | **"Your current customer base might reflect your past marketing efforts rather than your optimal audience."** [E — SparkToro, §9.5] |
| **Benchmark invention** | Supplying a conversion rate nobody measured | Never. `[NEEDS DATA]` |
| **Active-data-only pass** | The whole analysis rests on data the client handed you | That is operations data organised around products and customer attributes. Go get passive data (§10b) |
| **Big Hire mistaken for proof** | "They bought it, so the job is real" | Purchase proves persuasion. **Repeated use proves the job.** Ask for usage data |
| **False precision** | A score with two decimals and no visible inputs | Report gates, evidence level and assumption count alongside every number |
| **Averaging toward no one** | Splitting the difference between two real groups | The Air Force cockpit built for the average pilot fit **none** of them |

## §10b — The Three Fallacies of Innovation Data

Christensen's account of *how organisations lose the evidence they already had.* `[E — Competing Against Luck, ch. 8]` These are not research errors; they are drifts that happen to competent teams by default, which makes them worth naming explicitly.

### Fallacy 1 — Active data displaces passive data

**Passive data** is the unstructured context of the struggle: frustrations, workarounds, undesirable tradeoffs, nonconsumption. *"It has no voice or clear structure or champion or agenda... It's always present, but it isn't loud."*

**Active data** is what operations generate once you have customers: sales by product, customer attributes, productivity, competitive benchmarks. *"This data, as it turns out, is very loud. It shouts at you."*

The drift is organic and feels like maturity: teams *"migrate from the unstructured messiness of passive data to the reassuringly concrete active data,"* and start **managing the numbers instead of the job.** Note what active data organises itself around — **products and customer characteristics** — which are precisely the two things this skill refuses to define an audience by.

> *"In the early stage, managers are puzzle solvers, not number crunchers. Passive data does not broadcast itself loudly. You have to seek it out, put clues together, relentlessly ask why?"*

**In this skill:** passive data is what §9's escalation moves go and get. Active data arrives on its own. **A research pass that consulted only data someone handed you has consulted only active data**, and it will reproduce the client's existing view of their market.

### Fallacy 2 — Surface growth

Selling more products to existing customers has a tiny marginal cost and alluring margin, so companies copy or acquire products around them and end up doing **many jobs for many customers** — losing the focus that made them work.

The *New York Times* illustration: help readers unwind · deliver up-to-date news · help them become informed · help them fill time productively. Each additional job puts it against a specialist that does that one job better — *The Economist* for feeling informed in one weekly briefing, television for unwinding, the free *Metro* for filling a commute.

**In this skill:** this is the mechanism behind the "audience creep" red flag. A client serving four jobs is competing with four specialists and losing to each.

### Fallacy 3 — Conforming data

> *"The most calamitous failures of prediction usually have a lot in common. We focus on those signals that tell a story about the world as we would like it to be, not how it really is."* — Nate Silver

Sales knows what customers want because it talks to them. Marketing has ideas for leveraging the brand. R&D is excited about new capability. The BU head needs something that helps the P&L this year. **Each arrives with carefully constructed supporting data.** None is wrong; none is objective; and not one reflects the customer's job.

Gerald Zaltman's diagnosis: *"The tendency to treat facts as insights and leap directly from data to action... research is used to prove points rather than as fuel for imaginative insight."* And: *"A leader may think they've made a leap of faith based on clear data, but in reality, it's already been kind of preordained."*

### The underlying warning: all data is man-made

> *"Data is not the phenomena. The primary function of data is to represent the phenomena."*
> *"Deity does not create data and then bestow it upon mankind. All data is man-made. Somebody, at some point, decided what data to collect, how to organize it, how to present it, and how to infer meaning from it."*

The subjectivity of ethnographic research is obvious. **The subjectivity of numerical data "hides behind its superficial precision."** Christensen relays Tom Monahan's joke about endowing a **Museum of False Precision** — *"It promises to boast a well-stocked collection."*

**This is a warning aimed squarely at this skill's own machinery.** A TAS of `4.28` looks more authoritative than "this segment seems strong," and it is the same claim with two decimal places bolted on. That is exactly why the score is never reported without its gates, its evidence level and its assumption count — and why `E` is a variable inside the formula rather than a footnote.

**Also worth knowing before quoting anyone's financials:** revenue, cost and profit figures are themselves *"the result of processes of estimation, negotiation, debate, and politics in allocating overhead costs."* Their precision is a formatting convention.

### Countermeasures

| Fallacy | Countermeasure in this skill |
|---|---|
| Active over passive | Go and get passive data: nonconsumers, workarounds, refusers, community threads. **Never build a pass on handed-over data alone** |
| Surface growth | One job per segment object. Name the specialist competitor for each additional job the client serves |
| Conforming data | State the falsification condition **before** research. Require an unwelcome finding in every report |
| False precision | Score with sources per cell; report gates and assumption counts beside every number |

## §11 — Integration

- **Segment generation** (`segment-generation-lenses.md`): candidates may enter at L1. Generate generously.
- **Scoring** (`../templates/scoring-matrix-template.md`): **every cell carries a level.** The `E` (Evidence quality) variable in the TAS formula is the mean level across that segment's claims.
- **Segment object** (`../templates/segment-object-template.md`): `Evidence` and `Confidence` fields are populated directly from this ladder.
- **Avatar work** (`avatar-from-comments.md`): comment data tops out at **L3** on its own. Comments are one source type. To reach L4, add search data, job postings, or transactions.
- **VOC** (`voice-of-customer.md`): verbatim language is L2 by definition and reaches L3 by counting.
- **WTP** (`willingness-to-pay.md`): stated WTP is **L2–L3 at best**, regardless of sample size, because it is stated preference. Only observed prices and completed transactions reach L5–L6.
- **Final report** (`../templates/research-output-template.md`): the Evidence Profile block is mandatory.
