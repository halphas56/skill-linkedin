# The Customer Profile

**The structured artefact for recording an audience completely.** Where `jtbd-engine.md` is the *discovery method* — how to find the job — this file is the *recording instrument*: what a finished audience profile must contain, at what level of precision, and how to fill it systematically rather than by inspiration.

**Source:** Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda & Smith, *Value Proposition Design* (2014), read in the Russian edition. `[E]`

**Scoping note — deliberate.** Value Proposition Design is a two-sided canvas: the **Customer Profile** (circle) and the **Value Map** (square). This skill takes the circle. **The Value Map — products and services, pain relievers, gain creators — belongs downstream in `strong-offer-creator`.** Taking the whole canvas here would duplicate the offer skill and blur the handoff.

---

## §1 — Why the profile is a separate instrument from the job

JTBD tells you *what progress they seek in what circumstance*. That is the load-bearing insight, and it is one sentence.

**A profile is what you hand to a downstream team**, and one sentence is not enough for them to build against. The profile decomposes the job into three columns and then into sub-types, each of which drives a different downstream decision:

| Column | What it is | What it drives |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobs** | What they are trying to get done | Segment definition, message |
| **Pains** | What bothers them before, during and after — and what could go wrong | Objection handling, risk reversal, onboarding |
| **Gains** | The outcomes and benefits they want | The promise, the proof, the pricing logic |

**And the discipline that makes it useful:** every column is **ranked** by importance / severity / essentiality, and every entry is **quantified**. Both are covered below.

## §2 — Jobs

> *"К задачам относится то, что потребители хотят осуществить в своей профессиональной или личной жизни."* `[E]`

### The three main types

Already in this skill via `jtbd-engine.md` §5, restated here for completeness:

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Functional** | A concrete task or problem — mow the lawn, eat properly, write a report, serve a client professionally |
| **Social** | How they want to be perceived — a fashionable consumer, a respected professional |
| **Personal / emotional** | A desired emotional state — feeling secure about an investment, confident about job security |

### Supporting jobs — three roles this skill was missing

Customers also perform jobs connected to *acquiring and consuming* value, in three roles: `[E]`

| Role | Jobs in that role |
|---|---|
| **Value buyer** | Comparing offers · deciding to buy · queueing · paying · taking delivery |
| **Value co-creator** | Writing reviews · leaving feedback · participating in product development |
| **Value transferrer** | **Cancelling a subscription · disposing of the product · passing it on · reselling** |

**The transferrer role is the one nobody maps, and it is commercially significant.** End-of-life jobs drive:
- **Switching friction** — "what do I do with the old one?" was the entire blocker in Moesta's condo case (the dining room table) and appears in the mattress interview (*"I really do not want to leave our ten-year-old mattress out by the curb for all the neighbours to view"*)
- **Referral and resale** — a product that passes on well generates advocacy for free
- **Cancellation anxiety** — a pre-purchase objection about the exit, raised before the entry

**Ask it explicitly:** *"When you're done with this, what happens to it?"* The answer frequently contains an unaddressed pain worth more than anything in the functional column.

### Job context

> *"Задачи потребителя часто зависят от контекста, в котором они выполняются. Контекст может накладывать определенные ограничения."* `[E]`

Using a phone on a train differs from using it while driving. Going to the cinema with children differs from going with a partner. Restaurant diners apply **completely different criteria at lunch and at dinner**.

**Add contextual elements to the profile explicitly — they become constraints on everything downstream.**

> This converges with Category Entry Points (`category-entry-points.md`): the same person in a different situation is, for practical purposes, a different audience. Two frameworks, same conclusion.

### Job importance

Not all jobs matter equally. A job may be important because failing it has serious consequences, because it recurs frequently, or because it produces a strongly desired or strongly unwanted outcome. **Rank them.**

## §3 — Pains

> *"Это всё, что волнует потребителя до, во время и после выполнения задачи или просто мешает ее выполнить."* `[E]`

Note the span: **before, during and after.** Most pain research captures only "during."

### Three types — and they need different responses

This skill previously treated "pain" as one thing. The split is operationally important:

| Type | Definition | The right response |
|---|---|---|
| **Undesired outcomes & characteristics** | Functional (doesn't work, works badly, has side effects) · social ("I look bad doing this") · emotional ("I feel bad doing this") · ancillary ("annoying that I have to go to the shop for it"). Also disliked attributes — "boring to use", "ugly design" | Fix, or differentiate on it |
| **Obstacles** | What prevents starting or slows it down — *"I don't have time to do it properly"*, *"none of these options is affordable for me"* | **Removal.** An obstacle is not persuaded away |
| **Risks** | What could go wrong with serious consequences — *"if I do this I could lose credibility"*, *"a gap in our security would be a catastrophe"* | **Reassurance** — guarantees, proof, references, pilots |

**Obstacles and risks map directly onto the four forces** (`jtbd-engine.md` §6): obstacles are Habit-adjacent (friction of the present), risks are Anxiety. Confusing them wastes budget — you cannot reassure away an obstacle, and you cannot remove a fear.

### Pain severity

Pains are **extreme or moderate**. Rank them. A profile where everything is extreme has not been ranked.

### The question bank

Directly usable, from the source: `[E]`

- What does the customer consider **"too costly"** — takes a lot of time, costs too much, requires too much effort?
- What makes customers **feel bad**? What frustrates, annoys, or gives them a headache?
- **Why do existing value propositions not satisfy them?** What is missing? Which features annoy them, what shortcomings do they point out?
- What **main difficulties** do they face? Do they understand how it works, do they struggle to perform an action, or do they not want to perform it for some reason?
- What **negative social consequences** do they face or fear — losing face, influence, trust, status?
- What **risks** matter to them — financial, social, technical? Do they ask themselves what could go wrong?
- What **keeps them awake at night** — main worries, concerns, sources of anxiety?
- What **mistakes** do they most commonly make? Do they use existing solutions incorrectly?
- What **blocks adoption** — the need to pay upfront, lack of knowledge, some other barrier?

## §4 — Gains

> *"Выгода — это результаты и преимущества, которые желает получить потребитель."* `[E]`

### The four levels — a ladder, not a list

| Level | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Required** | Without it the solution doesn't work at all | A smartphone must be able to make a call |
| **Expected** | Substantial, though they could technically do without | An iPhone is expected to be convenient and stylish |
| **Desired** | Beyond expectation, would not be refused. **Surfaces when asked** | Compatibility with other devices |
| **Unexpected** | Beyond desired or expected; the customer doesn't even know | Touchscreens and the App Store before Apple shipped them |

**Why this ladder matters more than it looks.**

> **Required gains are table stakes — and they are exactly McDonald's non-discriminating features.** `[E — cross-source]` Something every competitor delivers cannot differentiate you, cannot be used to select between offers, and must be excluded from your positioning even though it must be present in your product.

That is the same conclusion Osterwalder reaches through *gain levels* and McDonald reaches through *KDF screening* (`market-mapping.md` §5). **Differentiation lives at desired and unexpected. Delivery obligation lives at required and expected.** Confusing the two produces marketing that boasts about a working telephone.

### The question bank

- What is most valuable in terms of **time, money and effort**?
- What **quality level** do they expect, and what do they want more or less of?
- What **attracts them** in existing value propositions? Which aspects please them? What performance and quality do they expect?
- What would **make life or work easier** — simpler onboarding, more services, lower cost of ownership?
- What **positive social effects** do they expect? What makes them look good, strengthens their position, raises their status?
- What do they **notice first** — design, guarantee, specific features, number of functions?
- What do they **dream about**? What would they like to achieve, or what would help most?
- **How do they measure success and failure?** What are their criteria for achievement or cost?
- What would make your offer **more attractive** — lower cost, less investment, lower risk, better quality?

## §5 — The three techniques that make the profile fillable

Most people can list a few jobs and then stall. These three fill the rest systematically.

### Technique 1 — Quantify everything

**This is the single sharpest instrument in the source.** `[E]`

> If a customer says *"waiting in a queue is a waste of time"* — **find out after how many minutes they start perceiving it as wasted.** Then the pain is defined as *"losing more than X minutes in a queue."*
>
> If a customer asks to *"improve performance"* — **ask what exactly they expect or want.** Then the gain is *"raising characteristic X above Y."*
>
> Instead of a vague gain *"a pay rise"* — **specify the amount they are aiming at.** Instead of *"takes too long"* — find out what "too long" means to this customer.

> *"Поняв, как потребитель измеряет выгоду, вы сможете включить в ценностное предложение наиболее подходящие факторы выгоды."*

**Why this belongs in an audience skill.** `voice-of-customer.md` §3 already requires you to capture *their* metric. This tells you **how to get it**: never accept an adjective. Every "too slow", "too expensive", "not reliable enough" has a number behind it, and the number is the specification for everything downstream — the promise, the proof, the price and the guarantee.

**It also converts a soft pain into a scoreable one.** "Waiting is annoying" cannot be ranked. "More than 4 minutes" can be measured, compared across segments, and used to size the economic value of the problem (`willingness-to-pay.md` §3).

### Technique 2 — The mirror

Pains and gains can be generated as **opposite formulations of the same job**: `[E]`

```
JOB:    "earn more money"
GAIN:   "a rise to £X"                    ← quantified
PAIN:   "a pay cut below £Y"              ← quantified mirror
        + OBSTACLE: "my employer doesn't give rises"
        + RISK:     "I won't be able to pay for my child's education"
```

**Procedure:** take each job → write the gain that fulfils it → write the pain that is its inverse → then *add* the obstacle blocking the job and the risk of failing it. Four entries generated from one job, systematically.

This is how a sparse profile becomes a full one without inventing anything.

### Technique 3 — Laddering with "why?"

> *"Задавайте вопрос «зачем?» многократно, пока не поймете точно, какие задачи стоят перед потребителем."* `[E]`

> Why does the customer want to learn a foreign language? Perhaps to improve their CV. Why improve the CV? Perhaps to earn more.

**Keep asking until you reach the job that actually drives them.** Superficial job understanding is the named danger here.

**How this pairs with the abstraction test** (`jtbd-engine.md` §3): laddering is the *procedure* for climbing; the abstraction test is the *stopping rule*. Ladder up until the alternatives on the candidate list come from different product categories — and stop before you reach a life theme nobody buys against.

## §6 — Ranking: the hierarchy

> *"Для разработки ценностного предложения, ориентированного на реальные потребности потребителя, необходимо четко представлять иерархию его задач, проблем и выгод."* `[E]`

Three ranked columns:

```
JOBS          PAINS            GAINS
important     extreme          essential
    ↕             ↕                ↕
insignificant moderate         nice-to-have
```

**Start from assumptions and test until the ranking reflects real priorities.** Understanding improves with every customer conversation and every experiment.

**The rule this skill adds:** an unranked profile is `L1` (`evidence-ladder.md`) regardless of how many items it contains. A list is not a finding; a *ranked* list, with the ranking sourced, is.

## §7 — The six profiling mistakes

Straight from the source, and several are not in this skill's existing red flags: `[E]`

| # | Mistake | Why it breaks the work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Several customer segments in one profile** | Produces a composite person who exists nowhere. The averaging error again |
| 2 | **Mixing jobs and outcomes** | Jobs are the work being done; gains are the results wanted. Merge them and neither is actionable |
| 3 | **Functional jobs at the expense of social and emotional** | *"Задача «хорошо выглядеть в глазах окружающих» может быть важнее задачи поиска удачного технического решения"* |
| 4 | **Listing jobs, pains and gains with an eye to your own offer** | **Conforming data applied to profiling.** You will find exactly the pains your product happens to relieve |
| 5 | **Too few items identified** | *"Хороший профиль потребителя должен быть заполнен стикерами"* — real customers face many pains and want many gains |
| 6 | **Vague descriptions of pains and gains** | Defeated by Technique 1 |

**Mistake 4 deserves special weight.** The instruction is to work **like ethnographers and temporarily forget what you offer**:

> *"издатель бизнес-литературы не должен замыкаться на тех задачах, проблемах и выгодах, которые связаны с книгами, поскольку у читателя есть выбор между книгами, консультантами, видео на YouTube или даже обучением по программе MBA."* `[E]`

**Note what that is.** A business-book publisher's real competitive set is books, consultants, YouTube and an MBA. **That is the abstraction test, stated a third time by a third independent source** — after Christensen (demand side) and McDonald (market-structure side). Three traditions, one rule: *the boundary is set by what the customer would substitute.*

## §8 — B2B: six stakeholder roles

Richer than this skill's existing buying-group model, and with one structural point that was missing entirely. `[E]`

| Role | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Influencers** | People or groups whose opinion is heeded — including managers, informally |
| **Recommenders** | Those who search, evaluate and issue a **formal recommendation** for or against |
| **Economic buyers** | Control the budget and execute the purchase. Focus on financial efficiency and savings |
| **Decision makers** | Responsible for the final choice and closing the deal; usually control budget |
| **End users** | Whoever ultimately receives the product or service. Passive or active depending on their role in the decision |
| **Saboteurs** | People or groups **obstructing** search, evaluation or acquisition |

### The structural point this skill was missing

> *"Управленцы обычно находятся внутри организации, в то время как авторитеты, советники, хозяйственники, конечные пользователи и противники могут находиться **как внутри организации, так и вне её**."* `[E]`

**Only the decision maker is reliably inside the buying organisation.** Everyone else may sit outside it:

- **Economic buyer outside:** a government paying for basic medical equipment in nursing homes. The home chooses; the state pays.
- **End users outside:** a device manufacturer buys chips for smartphones it sells to consumers. The chip vendor's end user is two steps away.
- **Recommender outside:** an external consultant, auditor, architect or agency whose specification decides the purchase.
- **Saboteur outside:** an incumbent supplier's account manager; a regulator; a parent company.

**This connects directly to the market map** (`market-mapping.md` §3). McDonald quantifies *where in the chain* decisions are made; VPD names *which role* each junction plays. Use them together: the map finds the junction, the role list tells you what that junction wants.

### It applies to B2C too

> A family buying a games console contains an economic buyer, an influencer, a decision maker, users and opponents. *"Есть смысл составить шаблон ценностного предложения для каждого из них."* `[E]`

**Build a profile per role, not per household.** The same is true of any purchase involving a partner, a parent, a child or a housemate.

## §9 — Multi-sided fit

Some business models only work as a **combination** of value propositions and segments. Each side needs its own fit. `[E]`

### Intermediary models
When a company sells through an intermediary it must satisfy **two customers** — the end user and the intermediary.

> *"Если она не создаст ясного ценностного предложения для посредника, продукт может вообще не достичь конечного потребителя."*

Haier sells appliances worldwide mostly through retailers like Carrefour and Walmart, and needs an attractive proposition for **both** the end buyer and the distributor.

### Platforms
Two or more sides interacting within one business model; **the platform exists only if all sides are present.** Airbnb needs one proposition for hosts and another for guests.

### The rule for this skill

**When the business model is intermediated or multi-sided, "the audience" is not one audience.** Produce a complete profile per side, and score each side separately in the matrix. A segment object covering only the end user in a channel business is an incomplete deliverable — say so rather than shipping it.

> Third convergence on the same point: McDonald's demand-pull warning (sell only to merchants and you are defenceless when your advantage is copied), the market map's junction quantification, and this. Three routes, one instruction: **profile the intermediary as seriously as the end user.**

## §10 — Test the circle before the square

**A sequencing rule this skill did not state, and it changes how validation is designed.** `[E]`

> *"Очень важно собрать доказательства до начала работы над ценностным предложением. Если начать с тестирования ценностного предложения, вы будете действовать вслепую, не зная, не отвергнут ли его потребители, не сконцентрировались ли вы на малозначимых задачах, проблемах и выгодах."*

| Test | What it is | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **The circle** | The Customer Profile | Which jobs, pains and gains genuinely matter most? |
| **The square** | The Value Map | Do customers care about *how* you propose to help? |
| **The rectangle** | The Business Model | Can this be delivered profitably and at scale? |

**Test in that order.** The reason is diagnostic, not procedural: **if you smoke-test an offer and it fails, you cannot tell whether the offer was wrong or the audience was wrong.** Testing the circle first disambiguates the failure.

Most validation advice — including much of the standard startup canon — jumps straight to the square. That is why so many "we tested it and it didn't work" conclusions are uninterpretable.

**In this skill's terms:** validation experiments (`../OPERATING-MANUAL.md` Step 12) should include at least one that tests *the audience profile itself* — not the message, not the offer — before any offer test is run.

### The three types of fit, mapped to the Evidence Ladder

| Fit | Achieved when | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem–solution** *(on paper)* | You have facts that customers have these jobs, pains and gains, and you've designed against them. **No evidence yet that anyone wants your solution** | `L3–L4` |
| **Product–market** *(in the market)* | Facts confirm your offer actually creates value and is gaining traction | `L5–L6` |
| **Business model** *(in the bank)* | Facts confirm it can be embedded in a profitable, scalable model | Economics proven |

Note the honesty built into the first row: problem–solution fit is explicitly *"на бумаге"* and *"соответствие пока не подтверждено фактами"*. **A profile plus a designed proposition is a hypothesis, however well-researched the profile is.**

## §11 — What this file does *not* cover

The right half of the canvas — **products and services, pain relievers, gain creators** — is offer design, not audience research. It belongs to `strong-offer-creator`.

**The handoff:** this skill delivers a ranked, quantified Customer Profile per segment and per stakeholder role. The offer skill builds the Value Map against it and checks fit. Do not build the Value Map here; you will duplicate the offer skill and the client will get two contradictory versions.

**One thing worth carrying across the boundary:** *"Не беспокойтесь, если вы отметили галочками не все проблемы/выгоды — удовлетворить их все невозможно."* `[E]` A profile is deliberately larger than any offer that answers it. Completeness in the profile is a virtue; completeness in the response is impossible.

## §12 — Quality gate

- [ ] **One profile per segment** — and per stakeholder role in B2B, per side in multi-sided models
- [ ] All three job types present — **social and emotional not left blank**
- [ ] **Supporting jobs** covered, including the **transferrer role** ("what happens when they're done with it?")
- [ ] **Context** recorded as an explicit constraint
- [ ] Pains split into **outcomes / obstacles / risks** — not lumped as "pain"
- [ ] Gains split across **required / expected / desired / unexpected**
- [ ] **Required gains flagged as table stakes** and excluded from differentiation claims
- [ ] **Every pain and gain quantified** — no adjectives standing in for numbers
- [ ] Mirror technique run — gain, inverse pain, obstacle, risk per job
- [ ] **"Why?" laddered** until the driving job is reached, stopping at the abstraction test
- [ ] All three columns **ranked**, with the ranking sourced
- [ ] Screened against the **six mistakes** — especially #4, listing with an eye to your own offer
- [ ] Profile is **full**, not sparse
- [ ] At least one validation experiment tests **the profile itself**, before any offer test
- [ ] Every entry carries an **evidence level**; an unranked or unquantified profile is `L1`

### The test

Read the profile and ask: **"Could someone who has never met this customer build the right thing from this alone — and would they know what to stop building?"**

If the profile contains no numbers, the answer is no.
