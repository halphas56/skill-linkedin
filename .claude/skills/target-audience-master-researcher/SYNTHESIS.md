# Synthesis

**Read this once before your first engagement. Re-read it when two sources seem to contradict each other, or when you cannot decide which instrument to reach for.**

> **One skill.** `SKILL.md` is the only entry point and the only file with frontmatter. Everything named below is a reference file it points at. Modules are files; the skill count is one.

This skill accumulated across seven research passes: an initial build, comment mining, a seventeen-point brief, three primary sources read in full — Christensen's *Competing Against Luck*, McDonald & Dunbar's *Market Segmentation*, Osterwalder's *Value Proposition Design* — then a pain-discovery brief that corrected an error rather than filling a gap. In between, **two rounds of adversarial testing found eight defects** — the second of which found that the first round's fix had inherited the flaw it was fixing. The result is **14 knowledge files, 53 framework cards, 13 source clusters and 13 test briefs — all under a single `SKILL.md`.**

**That is more than anyone will load.** This document exists to make the depth usable: what all of it is actually about, how the pieces nest, where they genuinely conflict, and what to do when you have four hours instead of four weeks.

Everything below marked `[S]` is synthesis — conclusions drawn across sources that no single author states. Attack them accordingly.

## 1 · The one law

Seven passes, four traditions, fifty-three frameworks. Underneath them is a single recurring error, and every instrument in this skill is a specific device for catching one version of it.

> **Data is not the phenomena. The primary function of data is to represent the phenomena.**
> — Christensen `[E]`

**The law:** *audience research is the practice of refusing convenient representations.* `[S]`

Every named failure in this corpus is the same substitution wearing a different costume:

| The substitution | The instrument that catches it |
|---|---|
| The **category name** for what the customer would actually do instead | The abstraction test · market definition by need |
| Your **feature list** for what actually discriminates | KDF screening · required-gain screening |
| The **average** for the people | The milkshake · the Air Force cockpit · the contradiction scan |
| **Attributes** for episodes | Cluster, don't segment |
| The **payer** for the decision | Buying roles · the market map |
| What they **say** for what they do | The Mom Test · the Evidence Ladder |
| **Engagement** for intent | Interest ≠ intent |
| A **demographic** for a circumstance | JTBD · a priori segmentation as "the gravest mistake" |
| The **loud data** for the true data | Active vs passive data |
| A **number with decimals** for a finding | The Museum of False Precision |

### The law has a second half, on the buyer's side

**Discovered when the pain module was built, and it is the most satisfying thing in this document.** `[S]`

Everything above describes the **researcher** substituting a representation for the thing. Witte's EPPM describes the **buyer** doing exactly the same:

> Faced with high threat and low efficacy, a person stops managing **the problem** and starts managing **the feeling about the problem** — defensive avoidance, denial, reactance. `[E]`

That is the identical error, on the other side of the table. The buyer accepts a representation (the fear) in place of the thing (the situation), because the thing looks unmanageable.

**Two consequences:**

1. **It explains why manipulation fails as a technique, not merely as a moral matter.** Fearmongering induces in the buyer precisely the substitution this entire skill exists to prevent in the analyst. It produces a person who is *managing their feelings about your ad*.
2. **It gives the ethical rule the same footing as every other rule here** — evidential, not moral. *Never raise threat without raising efficacy in the same breath*, because the alternative does not work.

**The symmetry, stated once:** the researcher who accepts a convenient representation gets a fictional audience; the buyer who is pushed into accepting one gets a fictional problem. **Same failure, both ends of the transaction.**

### And a third instance — the law turned on the skill itself

**Found in the second round of testing, and it is the least comfortable of the three.** `[S]`

Round one found that the skill routed at the beginning and validated at the end. The fix was a **premise check**: six client statements to challenge at intake.

Round two tested it against fresh briefs. **It missed three of six** — *"aggressive advertising converts well"*, *"the CFO decides"*, *"no search volume means no demand"*. All three are premise errors. None was on the list.

The list was not wrong. It was **a representation of a principle**, and I had accepted it in place of the principle.

> **A checklist is a representation of a rule. Accepting the checklist in place of the rule is the same substitution this skill exists to prevent — performed by the skill, on itself.**

The underlying principle was available the whole time and is the skill's own core protocol: **run the Evidence Ladder on the client's brief, not only on your findings.** Every one of the six rows is an instance of it. So are the three misses. Stated as a rule it generalises; enumerated as a list it fails the moment reality supplies a seventh case.

**The generalised failure, worth carrying:**

> **A fix built as an enumeration fails the same way the original did, one level up.**

**Three instances of one law, then:** the researcher substituting a representation for the market · the buyer substituting fear for the problem · **the instrument substituting a list for the principle that generated it.** The third is the hardest to see, because a checklist looks like rigour.

**The practical consequence for anyone extending this skill:** when you find a defect and reach for a checklist to prevent it, **ask what rule generates that checklist** and write the rule down too. The list is worked examples. The rule is the fix.

**Why this matters operationally.** When you meet a situation none of the 53 cards covers — and you will — the generative question is not "which framework applies?" It is:

> **"What representation am I about to accept in place of the thing itself?"**

That question reconstructs most of this skill from first principles. `[S]`

## 2 · The altitude stack

The frameworks are not parallel alternatives. They operate at **five different zoom levels**, and each answers a question the others cannot. `[S]`

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1 · MARKET STRUCTURE    Where in the chain is it decided?    │  market-mapping.md
│                          Who controls what share?            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2 · SITUATION           When does the category come to mind? │  category-entry-points.md
│                          Which moments create demand?        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3 · PERSON              What progress, in what circumstance? │  jtbd-engine.md
│                          What forces block the switch?       │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4 · PROFILE             What must be written down, ranked    │  customer-profile.md
│                          and quantified?                     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5 · SENTENCE            What words did they actually use?    │  voice-of-customer.md
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The characteristic failure at each entry point

**Most audience research fails not because a framework was wrong, but because the researcher entered at one altitude and never moved.** `[S]`

| Enter and stay at | You get | You miss | Diagnostic symptom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market structure only** | A map of who decides | Why anyone buys | You know the channel and have nothing to say to it |
| **Situation only** | A list of occasions | Depth on any one of them | Many angles, no positioning |
| **Person only** | A rich struggling moment | The chain around them | You research end users in a market where specifiers decide |
| **Profile only** | A tidy artefact | Whether it was ever true | A beautiful canvas at `L1` |
| **Sentence only** | Excellent copy | The right person to send it to | High engagement, no revenue |

McDonald's own method demonstrates the trap from the inside: his process is superb on structure (levels 1–2) and has to reach for *"understand their real needs — why they buy"* as step 5 precisely because structure alone cannot produce motivation. **Christensen supplies exactly what McDonald's step 5 needs, and McDonald supplies exactly what Christensen's method lacks — the chain.** `[S]`

**The rule: traverse, don't camp.** A defensible pass touches at least three altitudes.

**Two modules deliberately sit across the stack rather than at one level:** `knowledge/evidence-ladder.md` (every altitude, one question) and the pain pair (altitudes 3–5, all four questions). Both are described by the second axis below, not by this one.

### What each column costs you when skipped

| Column | Skip it and you get |
|---|---|
| **Is it real?** | **Fiction, written fluently.** The failure mode the Evidence Ladder exists for |
| **What is it, exactly?** | Vagueness. A finding nobody can act on |
| **Is it worth money?** | **Applause without revenue.** Real pain, real people, no budget |
| **May we say it?** | Manipulation — which, per EPPM, also does not work |

### This explains the skill's own history

Three defects, one diagnosis each: `[S]`

| Defect found | Diagnosis on this map |
|---|---|
| Early versions produced enthusiastic, unmonetisable segments | **Column 3 was nearly empty.** Only added with `willingness-to-pay.md`, then `pain-opportunity.md` |
| The pain module shipped with zero ethics coverage | **Column 4 was literally empty.** One occupant now, and it is still the thinnest column |
| The single PPS score was wrong | It **collapsed columns 2 and 3 into one number** — *what is it* averaged with *is it worth money* |

**The skill did not grow randomly. It grew along two axes while I was tracking one**, which is why it felt like accumulation rather than construction.

### The operational rule

> **Every finding gets all four questions, in order. Most engagements ask the first two thoroughly and skip the last two entirely.**

Column 3 is where a good analysis becomes a business case. **Column 4 is where it stays defensible** — and it is the one nobody asks for, which is precisely why it has to be built in rather than requested.

### Where pain sits, and why it needed two files

Pain is the only subject in the skill that **occupies all four columns**, which is why one file could not hold it:

| File | Columns |
|---|---|
| `knowledge/pain-discovery.md` | is it real? · what is it? · may we say it? |
| `knowledge/pain-opportunity.md` | **is it worth money?** |

Splitting them was not filing convenience. **It is the same distinction the two pain scores make** — and collapsing it is the error the single PPS committed.

## 3 · The dependency chain, and the universal failure

Put every sequencing rule in the corpus end to end and a strict dependency appears:

```
market boundary → where decisions happen → whose struggle → recorded profile
                → their words → the offer → the message
```

Now notice something. **Each author independently complains that practitioners start too far to the right — and none of them notices the others said the same thing.** `[S]`

| Author | The complaint | Where they say people start |
|---|---|---|
| **McDonald** | *"Positioning is easy. Market segmentation is difficult. **Positioning problems stem from poor segmentation**"* | At the message |
| **Osterwalder** | *"If you start by testing the value proposition, you'll be acting blind"* | At the offer |
| **Christensen** | Companies *"manage the numbers instead of the job"* | At the metrics |
| **Dunford** | Teams list *"phantom competitors"* they never lose to | At the competitor set |
| **Revella** | Persona training that begins with *"choose a name and a photo"* | At the person |

**The synthesis: the most common failure in audience work is not a wrong answer at a step. It is starting at step five.** `[S]`

That is diagnosable in one question at intake:

> **"What is the earliest link in that chain you haven't actually established with evidence?"**

Start there, whatever the client asked for. If they came for messaging help and their market definition is wrong, the messaging work will be wasted — and this is the single most common shape of a wasted engagement.

## 4 · The two engines, and how each one lies to you

There are exactly two ways to produce segments, they need different evidence, and **they fail in opposite directions.** `[S]`

| | **Top-down** | **Bottom-up** |
|---|---|---|
| Motion | Propose, then eliminate | Observe, then cluster |
| Instruments | Generation lenses · CEP · Different-X · noncustomer tiers | Micro-segments · ABCDX · PMF segmentation · CRM analysis |
| Produces | 10–20 candidate hypotheses | 30–50 observed combinations → 7–10 clusters |
| Needs | Almost nothing | Sales data, volumes, often commissioned research |
| Entry evidence | `L1` | `L3`+ |
| **Fails by** | **Plausible fiction** — twenty segments, all invented, scored into false precision | **Path dependence** — you can only find segments among people who already bought |

### Why neither is safe alone

**Top-down's failure is the one this skill is most exposed to**, because an LLM generates plausible segments effortlessly. Twenty candidates with a scoring matrix looks like rigour and can be entirely fictional. The Evidence Ladder exists specifically to make that visible.

**Bottom-up's failure is subtler and better disguised**, because it looks empirical. But SparkToro names it exactly:

> *"Your current customer base might reflect your past marketing efforts rather than your optimal audience."* `[E]`

Cluster your existing customers and you will rediscover your own history. Every noncustomer — Blue Ocean's three tiers, Christensen's nonconsumption, the people who suffer two years before buying an incontinence product — is invisible to it by construction.

**The resolution:** `[S]`

> **Top-down keeps bottom-up from mistaking its own history for the market. Bottom-up keeps top-down from mistaking its own fluency for evidence.**

Run both when you can. When you can only run one, name which failure mode you are exposed to, in the deliverable.

## 5 · Two axes of commitment

The Evidence Ladder measures how much **the market** has committed. McDonald's fourth checklist question measures how much **the client** will commit. These are independent, and together they predict whether an engagement produces anything. `[S]`

```
                    CLIENT WILL ACT
                    low            high
                ┌──────────────┬──────────────┐
        high    │  SHELF-WARE  │   THE WIN    │
   MARKET       │  Correct and │  Evidenced   │
   EVIDENCE     │  ignored     │  and acted   │
   (L0–L6)      ├──────────────┼──────────────┤
        low     │   THEATRE    │  DANGEROUS   │
                │  Nobody was  │  They'll act │
                │  ever going  │  confidently │
                │  to act      │  on fiction  │
                └──────────────┴──────────────┘
```

**The quadrant nobody names is the bottom right, and it is the one this skill can actually cause.** `[S]` A fluent, well-formatted, confidently-scored audience report handed to a client eager to reorganise around it — built on `L1` guesses — does more damage than no report at all. The client acts, spends, and attributes the failure to execution.

That is why the skill refuses to report a TAS without its evidence level and assumption count beside it, and why any `L0` in a delivered document is treated as a defect rather than a rough edge.

**Both axes are measurable at intake.** Ask the evidence question and the *"will you actually change the product, the pricing, the sales script and the channel?"* question in the same conversation, and you know which quadrant you are heading for before doing any work.

## 6 · The convergence map

Where independent traditions agree, weight the conclusion more heavily than any single author. Where only one source speaks, say so. `[S]`

### Three-way corroborated — treat as settled

**The boundary rule.** The market is bounded by what the customer would substitute, not by what the category is called.
- Christensen, from demand: the abstraction test — if every candidate is in one product category, it is a spec, not a job
- McDonald, from market structure: *"the aggregation of all products or services which customers regard as being capable of satisfying the same need"*
- Osterwalder, from offer design: work like ethnographers and forget what you sell — the reader chooses books, consultants, YouTube or an MBA

Three traditions, three methods, three decades. **Nothing else in this corpus is corroborated this heavily.**

### Multiply corroborated — high confidence

| Conclusion | Independent sources |
|---|---|
| **Never average two real groups** | Christensen (milkshake · Air Force cockpit) · McDonald (*"head in the oven, feet in the fridge"*) · Ramanujam ($20/$100 → $60 loses both) |
| **The buyer is not the user** | Osterwalder (six roles; only the decision maker is reliably internal) · McDonald (customers ≠ consumers; junctions) · Gartner (6–10 people) · CEP (With/For Whom) |
| **Stated preference is weak evidence** | Fitzpatrick (Mom Test) · SparkToro (*"people are terrible at reporting their own behavior"*) · Christensen (Rowland's focus group was wrong) · Van Westendorp's own caveat |
| **Refusal is mandatory** | See §8 — six sources |

### Two-way corroborated

| Conclusion | Sources |
|---|---|
| **Table stakes cannot differentiate** | McDonald (KDF screening) · Osterwalder (required + expected gains) — the same object from opposite directions |
| **Cluster, don't slice** | Christensen (deconstructing episodes destroys meaning) · McDonald (micro-segments → 7–10 clusters) |
| **Commoditisation is usually self-inflicted** | McDonald (segmentation failure forces price competition) · Christensen ("commodity" is not inevitable on the lifecycle) |

### Added in pass seven

| Conclusion | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency is not importance** — high-priority needs are *not* mentioned more often than low-priority ones | Griffin & Hauser, *Marketing Science* 12(1), 1993 | `[E]` peer-reviewed, and it invalidates the default behaviour of counting mentions |
| **Threat without efficacy produces rejection**, not persuasion — defensive avoidance, denial, reactance | Witte, EPPM; Witte & Allen meta-analysis | `[E]` — and it makes the ethical line and the effectiveness line the same line |
| **Persuasion preserves full information, emotional space and a clear exit** | Dark-patterns / autonomy literature; TARES | `[E]` — testable, which is why it was adopted over the values list |

### Single-source — useful, not settled

Flagged so they are not mistaken for consensus: the **TAS weights** (a design decision, uncalibrated) · **CEP's 3C screen** · **the ABCDX letters** · **the comment-mining stimulus map** (my own synthesis) · **the Opportunity Algorithm's numeric bands** · every **conversion benchmark** in existence, which is why this skill contains none.

## 7 · The conflict map

Three places where sources genuinely disagree. Do not smooth these over; route between them.

### Conflict 1 — Focus vs. reach

**Sharp / Ehrenberg-Bass:** brands grow by reaching more category buyers, especially light ones; rival customer bases are statistically interchangeable.
**Everyone else in this corpus:** narrow, pick a beachhead, don't straddle.

**Partial resolution.** `[S]` The two operate on different objects:

| | Applies to | Instruction |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | **The offer** | One segment, one job. Don't sit between two |
| **Breadth** | **Memory** | Attach the brand to as many entry situations as possible |

You can be narrow in what you *sell* and broad in when you are *remembered* — which is exactly why CEP, a breadth instrument from the breadth school, is usable here for a focus decision.

**Where the resolution does not hold:** Sharp also argues against narrow *targeting* in media buying, not only against narrow branding. That part remains a real disagreement. **Route by regime** — challenger with no distribution → focus; established mass brand with physical availability solved → reach. Name the regime in the deliverable.

### Conflict 2 — Depth vs. prevalence

**Christensen:** *"a huge number of data points from a smaller sample size"* — insights are stories, not statistics; sample size of one, repeated.
**McDonald:** 30–50 micro-segments, quantified, cluster-analysed.

**Resolution:** they answer different questions. Depth answers *why*; prevalence answers *how many*. `[S]` A `L4` claim needs both, which is what the independence test in the Evidence Ladder is enforcing — one stated source, one behavioural, one transactional.

### Conflict 3 — The milkshake itself

Strategyn (Ulwick) argues Christensen's milkshake violates rigorous JTBD segmentation.

**Resolution:** present the milkshake as an **illustration** of why demographics fail, never as a **method**. When you need method, use the six-link chain and the abstraction test. Both files already say this.

## 8 · The refusal principle

Six sources, six routes, one conclusion. `[S]`

| Source | The refusal |
|---|---|
| **Christensen** | *"Signal 'this product is not for you' or they'll come back and say it's a crummy product"* |
| **McDonald** | *"Select a segment and serve it. Do not straddle segments and sit between them"* |
| **Osterwalder** | *"Don't worry if you haven't ticked every pain — satisfying them all is impossible"* |
| **ABCDX** | The D-segment: revenue that costs more than it pays |
| **Dunford** | Best-fit customers care *a lot*; serving "just OK" customers spends what evangelists earned |
| **Aulet** | A beachhead means everything else is deferred, explicitly |

**The synthesis, and it reframes the whole deliverable:** `[S]`

> **An audience is defined by the refusal, not by the selection.**

Anyone can name a group they would like to sell to. The strategic content is entirely in what is given up. This explains three things at once:

- Why *"everyone with this problem"* is the canonical failure — it excludes nobody, so it decides nothing
- Why the Anti-ICP is harder to write than the ICP — **the ICP is a wish; the anti-ICP is a decision**
- Why *"every recommendation names a trade-off"* is a non-negotiable rule rather than a stylistic preference

If a deliverable contains no refusal, no choice has been made yet, however many pages it runs to.

## 10 · The minimum viable pass

**This skill is built for depth and most engagements do not have it.** A four-hour version that is honest beats a four-week version nobody runs. `[S]`

### Four hours — the triage pass
1. **Market definition by need** — run the substitution test. 20 min.
2. **Six-link chain** for the client's assumed audience. Mark every link `L0`–`L6`. 45 min.
3. **The list test** — where are 200 of them, today? 30 min.
4. **Review or comment mining**, 20–30 artefacts, verbatim. 90 min.
4b. **One pain, both directions.** Ladder the loudest complaint up to its root; chain it down to a business consequence. Record the highest **behavioural rung** — did anyone build a workaround, pay, or hire? 30 min.
5. **One unexpected candidate** from the generators. 20 min.
6. **Deliver:** the weakest link in the chain, the evidence profile, and the single cheapest experiment that would move the load-bearing claim up one level.

**Output shape:** one page. The finding is usually *"your audience definition is missing a `when`, and here is how to get it in a week."*

### One day — add
Habitat map with named venues · four competitor quote patterns · **cost of inaction with the arithmetic shown** · anti-ICP · TAS on 5–8 candidates with sources per cell.

### One week — add
5–10 interviews (switch or problem) · WTP triangulation · **both pain scores with the quadrant named** · full segment objects for the top 3 · positioning hypotheses with the swap test · a validation plan that tests the circle before the square · **the ethical review on any pain-based message**.

### One month or more — add
The market map, quantified · 30–50 micro-segments clustered · parallel smoke tests · the bottom-up engine cross-checked against the top-down candidates · **the Pain × Segment matrix, and the concentration / threshold / evolution questions fed back into segmentation**.

**The rule:** never run a longer pass than the client's decision deserves, and never present a shorter pass as though it were a longer one.

## 11 · What this skill is still weak at

Stated plainly so it can be improved rather than discovered by a client. `[S]`

- **Established mass-consumer brands.** The whole apparatus is tuned for focus. The regime check mitigates; it does not solve.
- **Genuinely new markets.** Search data, review mining, competitor analysis and micro-segments all fail when the category does not exist. Falls back to interviews and analogous markets.
- **Very large enterprises.** Buying complexity beyond 6–10 people, procurement, multi-year cycles.
- **Segment attractiveness scoring.** The TAS weights are a specified design, not a calibrated model. No outcome dataset exists in the corpus to validate them, and McDonald's Directional Policy Matrix material was not obtained.
- **The accumulation risk.** Twelve knowledge files, and a real engagement will load two. **Instruments have been added faster than judgement about when not to use them** — §10 is the countermeasure, and it is thinner than the instruments it governs.
- **Its own top-down bias.** An LLM produces plausible segments effortlessly. Every guardrail in the Evidence Ladder exists because of this, and none of them is self-enforcing.
- **Enforcement is partial, and the earlier diagnosis here was half wrong.** This section previously read *"no amount of further writing fixes it."* That was too pessimistic, and the correction is worth stating because it generalises. `[S]`

  **The guardrails that survived** — evidence profile, assumption count, gates — all lived in **templates**. The ones that kept getting skipped — the premise audit, the unwelcome finding — lived **only in prose**. The difference was never importance:

  > **A rule in prose must be remembered. A rule in a form must be filled or visibly skipped.**

  The **Delivery Header** (`templates/research-output-template.md` §0) moves the remaining prose-only guardrails into structure, and rubric gate **G6** makes its absence a test failure. **A blank in a form is visible; a forgotten paragraph is not.**

  **What remains genuinely unenforced:** filling the header is still a procedure. The header does not execute itself — it converts a *silent* failure into a *visible* one, which is a large improvement and not a solution. **Whether it survives a session that has not been reminded is exactly what `tests/COLD-RUN.md` exists to find out.**

  **The cold run has now happened** (`tests/run-log-2026-08-27-cold.md`), and it answered a different question than the one asked. **The skill never loaded** — zero tool calls — so what got measured was the model without the skill, not the header without a reminder. **The header question stays open.**

  What the run did establish is worth more:

  - **The skill's ethical *conclusions* were reached without the skill.** Refusal quality 3/3, validation design 3/3, premise timing 3/3. A capable model refuses shame framing, names a trade-off and challenges an unevidenced client claim on its own.

### What that one test round changed structurally

Worth recording here, because it altered a principle rather than a detail. `[S]`

The three High-severity defects shared a root cause:

> **The skill routed at the beginning and validated at the end, with nothing checking the client's premise in between.**

Mode selection accepted every brief as stated. The red-flags checklist — which contains the right challenges — ran *"before delivering."* So a false premise produced a complete, competent analysis, and the error surfaced at the delivery gate with the work already spent.

**The correction, now a sixth principle alongside those in §1–§5:**

> **Validation belongs at the front, not only at the back. A premise error caught at intake costs one question; the same error caught at the delivery gate costs the engagement.**

This is why `OPERATING-MANUAL.md` §3 now carries a six-statement premise check that runs **before** mode selection, and why RESEARCH PLAN is a mode selected at intake rather than a status discovered at the end.

**A related finding, about measurement rather than method:** the fix improved the skill and **moved no rubric score**, because the rubric measured output quality and was blind to work wasted reaching it. Two runs can produce identical documents while one wasted the engagement. Dimension D11 was added in response. **An evaluation instrument that cannot see its own blind spot is the same failure this skill warns about everywhere else — a representation accepted in place of the thing.**
